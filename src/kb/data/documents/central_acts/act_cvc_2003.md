# Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003 (Act No. 45 of 2003)

## 1. Statutory Identity & Legislative Purpose
- **Full Title**: The Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003
- **Enactment Date**: Assented to by the President on 11 September 2003.
- **Nodal Ministry**: Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT), Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions.
- **Historical Genesis**:
  - Established originally in **February 1964** on the recommendations of the **K. Santhanam Committee on Prevention of Corruption (1962–1964)** as an executive resolution body.
  - Conferred **statutory status** following the landmark Supreme Court ruling in **Vineet Narain v. Union of India (1998)** (Jain Hawala Case), where the Supreme Court directed that CVC be made an autonomous statutory body with superintendence over the Delhi Special Police Establishment (DSPE/CBI).
- **Preamble & Objective**: An Act to provide for the constitution of a Central Vigilance Commission to inquire or cause inquiries to be conducted into offences alleged to have been committed under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 by certain categories of public servants of the Central Government, corporations established under Central Acts, Government companies, societies and other local authorities owned or controlled by the Central Government and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

---

## 2. Composition & Appointment (Sections 3 & 4)
- **Composition (Section 3)**:
  - **Central Vigilance Commissioner** (Chairperson);
  - Not more than **two Vigilance Commissioners** (Members).
  - Selected from persons who have been or are in civil service of the Union or all-India service, or persons with experience in finance, law, banking, insurance, or vigilance administration.
- **Selection Committee (Section 4)**:
  - Appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal on the recommendation of a Committee consisting of:
    1. **Prime Minister** (Chairperson);
    2. **Minister of Home Affairs** (Member);
    3. **Leader of the Opposition in the House of the People** (Lok Sabha) (or leader of largest single opposition group).
- **Term & Conditions of Service (Section 5)**:
  - **Term**: **4 years** from the date of entering office or until attaining the age of **65 years**, whichever is earlier (shorter than standard 5-year commissions).
  - **Ineligibility for Further Employment**: On ceasing to hold office, CVC and Vigilance Commissioners are ineligible for any diplomatic assignment, appointment as Governor, or any other appointment under the Government of India or Government of a State.
- **Removal (Section 6)**:
  - Removed by President only on ground of **proved misbehaviour or incapacity** after the Supreme Court, on a reference made by the President, has on inquiry reported that the Commissioner ought to be removed (similar to UPSC/Supreme Court judges).

---

## 3. Functions & Powers of CVC (Section 8)
- **Key Functions (Section 8(1))**:
  1. **Superintendence over CBI / DSPE**:
     - Exercise superintendence over the functioning of the Delhi Special Police Establishment (DSPE) insofar as it relates to the **investigation of offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (PCA)**.
     - *Crucial Caveat (Section 8(2))*: CVC **cannot** direct CBI to investigate or dispose of any particular case in a particular manner (investigative autonomy of investigating officers).
  2. **Inquiry into Corruption Allegations**:
     - Inquire or cause an inquiry/investigation to be conducted on a reference made by the Central Government against high-level public servants.
     - Inquire into any complaint against:
       - Members of All India Services serving in connection with Union affairs;
       - Group 'A' officers of the Central Government;
       - Specified level officers in Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs), Public Sector Banks (Scale V and above), RBI, NABARD, SIDBI, and LIC.
  3. **Review of CBI Progress**:
     - Review the progress of investigations conducted by DSPE into corruption offences.
     - Review the progress of applications pending with competent authorities for **sanction of prosecution** under PCA 1988.
  4. **Advice to Central Government**:
     - Tender advice to Central Government, corporations, and PSUs on vigilance matters and disciplinary proceedings.
- **Powers of Civil Court (Section 11)**:
  - Summons and enforcing attendance of persons;
  - Discovery and production of documents;
  - Receiving evidence on affidavits;
  - Requisitioning public records from any court or office.

## 4. Single Directive & Striking Down of Section 6A
- **The "Single Directive" History**:
  - Executive rule requiring prior approval of the Central Government before conducting any inquiry or investigation into corruption allegations against officers of the rank of Joint Secretary and above.
  - Struck down in *Vineet Narain (1998)*, but reinserted into CVC Act 2003 as **Section 6A** and into DSPE Act as Section 6A.
- **Subramanian Swamy v. Director, CBI (2014) (5-Judge Constitution Bench)**:
  - Supreme Court unanimously struck down Section 6A of the DSPE Act / CVC Act as unconstitutional and violative of **Article 14** (Equality before law).
  - Held: Corruption by a public servant is a crime against society. Status, rank, or hierarchy cannot create an artificial class of public servants shielded from swift, unannounced criminal investigation.
- **2018 PCA Amendment (Section 17A Response)**:
  - Parliament subsequently inserted Section 17A into PCA 1988 requiring prior police inquiry sanction for decisions taken in discharge of official functions, reviving debates on executive immunity.

---

## 5. Whistleblower Protection Mandate (PIDRE Resolution & Law)
- In 2004, following the murder of NHAI whistleblower Satyendra Dubey, the Supreme Court directed the Government to create an interim mechanism.
- Central Government issued the **Public Interest Disclosure and Protection of Informers (PIDRE) Resolution, 2004**, designating the **Central Vigilance Commission as the Designated Agency** to receive whistleblower complaints.
- **Powers under PIDRE**: CVC can protect informant identity, order police protection, and initiate action against authorities victimizing whistleblowers.
- (Whistleblowers Protection Act, 2014 was passed by Parliament but delayed in full operational notification).

---

## 6. Chief Vigilance Officers (CVOs) & Institutional Architecture
- **CVO System**: CVC operates through Chief Vigilance Officers (CVOs) appointed in every Ministry, Department, and PSU.
- **Dual Role of CVO**:
  - Serves as the special assistant to the Secretary/Head of Department on vigilance matters;
  - Acts as an extended arm and reporting link to the CVC.
- **Appointment of CVO**: Prior consultation and concurrence of CVC is mandatory for appointing CVOs in Ministries and PSUs.

---

## 7. Critical Limitations & UPSC Answer Points
1. **Advisory Nature**: CVC is largely an **advisory body**; it recommends disciplinary actions or criminal prosecution to ministries, but ministries can differ from or dilute CVC advice (though deviations must be reported in CVC's Annual Report laid before Parliament under Section 14).
2. **No Power to Prosecute Directly**: CVC does not have its own prosecution wing; must depend on CBI or state police forces to register FIRs and charge sheets.
3. **Limited Cadre & Resources**: Heavy dependence on deputation staff from civil services.
4. **Jurisdictional Ceiling**: Does not cover private sector bribery, non-executive MPs, or judges of superior courts.

## 8. UPSC High-Yield Answer Framing & Keywords
- **Core Pillars**: Apex vigilance body, Institutional integrity, Preventive vigilance, Punitive vigilance, Surveillance & detection, Systemic reforms.
- **High-Yield Concepts**:
  - Santhanam Committee (1964) genesis
  - *Vineet Narain (1998)* statutory transformation
  - Section 8(1) superintendence over CBI vs. Section 8(2) non-interference in investigation
  - 4-year / 65-year tenure (Section 5)
  - Section 6A striking down in *Subramanian Swamy (2014)*
  - Preventive vigilance mechanisms: Integrity Pacts, e-procurement, rotation of officers in sensitive posts, Vigilance Awareness Week.
