# Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992)

- **Citation**: 1992 Supp (2) SCC 651; AIR 1993 SC 412
- **Decided On**: February 18, 1992
- **Bench Strength**: 5-Judge Constitution Bench
- **Bench Members**: L.M. Sharma, M.N. Venkatachaliah, J.S. Verma, K. Jayachandra Reddy, and S.C. Agrawal JJ.
- **Constitutional Provisions Interpreted**: Tenth Schedule (Anti-Defection Law), Article 102(2), Article 191(2), Article 122, Article 212, Article 136, Article 226, 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act 1985

---

## 1. Background & Core Facts
In 1985, the 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act added the Tenth Schedule to curb the evil of political defections ("Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram" culture). Paragraph 7 of the Tenth Schedule explicitly barred the jurisdiction of all courts in respect of any matter connected with the disqualification of a member of a House under the Schedule. Paragraph 6 gave the Speaker/Chairman sole and final authority to adjudicate disqualification petitions. A batch of petitions challenged the constitutional validity of the Tenth Schedule, arguing that it violated the Basic Structure (democracy and judicial review) and freedom of speech of legislators under Article 19(1)(a) and 105.

---

## 2. Key Constitutional Questions
1. Does the Tenth Schedule violate freedom of speech and conscience of elected legislators?
2. Is Paragraph 7 (barring judicial review) constitutionally valid, and did its enactment require ratification by State Legislatures under Article 368(2)?
3. What is the legal status and nature of the Speaker/Chairman while adjudicating disqualification petitions?
4. When can courts intervene in anti-defection proceedings?

---

## 3. Majority Ratio Decidendi (3-2 Majority)
1. **Constitutional Validity of the Tenth Schedule Upheld**:
   The Court upheld the overall constitutional validity of the Tenth Schedule, holding that it serves a vital public purpose of ensuring political stability, preventing unethical floor-crossing, and strengthening parliamentary democracy.

2. **Paragraph 7 Struck Down (Judicial Review Reaffirmed)**:
   The Court unanimously held that **Paragraph 7 was unconstitutional** because it curtailed the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and High Courts under Articles 136, 226, and 227 without securing the mandatory ratification by half of the State Legislatures under the proviso to Article 368(2).

3. **Speaker Acts as a Quasi-Judicial Tribunal**:
   The Court held that the Speaker or Chairman, while determining disqualification petitions under Paragraph 6, is **not functioning as the House itself, but as a statutory Tribunal**. Consequently, orders passed by the Speaker are subject to **judicial review** on grounds of:
   - Jurisdictional error
   - Violation of constitutional mandates
   - Malafides
   - Non-compliance with rules of natural justice
   - Perversity

4. **Timing of Judicial Intervention (No Quo Warranto at Interlocutory Stage)**:
   Judicial review cannot be invoked at an interlocutory stage prior to the Speaker making an order. The courts will not interfere while the proceedings are pending before the Speaker, but will exercise review once a final decision is rendered.

---

## 4. Key Dissenting Opinion (Minority - J.S. Verma and L.M. Sharma JJ.)
The minority held that the Speaker is an active partisan member of a political party and dependent on the ruling party's continuous support; therefore, vesting exclusive and final adjudicatory power in the Speaker violates the Basic Structure of democracy. They recommended an independent tribunal or the Election Commission.
