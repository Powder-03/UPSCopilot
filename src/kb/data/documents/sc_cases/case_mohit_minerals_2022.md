# Union of India v. Mohit Minerals Pvt. Ltd. (2022)

- **Citation**: (2022) 10 SCC 700; 2022 INSC 598
- **Decided On**: May 19, 2022
- **Bench Strength**: 3-Judge Bench
- **Bench Members**: D.Y. Chandrachud, Surya Kant, and A.S. Bopanna JJ.
- **Constitutional Provisions Interpreted**: Article 246A, Article 279A, Article 280, 101st Constitutional Amendment Act 2016, Cooperative Federalism, Fiscal Federalism

---

## 1. Background & Core Facts
Mohit Minerals challenged notifications issued by the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) imposing an integrated goods and services tax (IGST) on ocean freight paid by foreign exporters for transport of goods to India. The Gujarat High Court struck down the notifications as exceeding statutory powers under the IGST Act, 2017. The Union Government appealed to the Supreme Court, contending that the levy had been recommended by the GST Council under Article 279A, and therefore possessed binding legislative force on all levels of government.

---

## 2. Key Constitutional Questions
1. Are recommendations made by the GST Council under Article 279A binding on Parliament and State Legislatures?
2. What is the nature and constitutional architecture of Article 246A regarding the simultaneous power of taxation?
3. How does the 101st Constitutional Amendment redefine Indian fiscal federalism?

---

## 3. Ratio Decidendi
1. **GST Council Recommendations are Persuasive, Not Binding**:
   The Supreme Court authoritatively held that **the recommendations of the GST Council are not binding on either Parliament or the State Legislatures**. They possess only persuasive value. 
   - Article 279A(4) uses the word *"recommendations"*, which cannot be construed to mean "commands" or "mandates".
   - If the recommendations were legally binding, it would undermine the sovereign legislative powers granted to State Legislatures under Article 246A and erode parliamentary democracy.

2. **Simultaneous Legislative Power (Article 246A)**:
   Article 246A creates a **unique constitutional innovation**: it gives simultaneous and equal legislative power to both the Union Parliament and State Legislatures to legislate on goods and services tax. Unlike the standard List II (State List) or List I (Union List) model under Article 246, Article 246A does not provide for Union supremacy or general repugnancy under Article 254.

3. **Cooperative and Uncooperative Federalism**:
   Justice Chandrachud articulated that Indian federalism is a **"continuous dialogue"** between two sovereign constitutional entities:
   > *"Indian federalism is a dialogue between two sovereign entities. The dynamic between the Union and the States is not always harmonious, but can also encompass 'uncooperative federalism' where states challenge central assertions to negotiate collaborative consensus."*

4. **Preservation of State Fiscal Autonomy**:
   While the GST Council serves as an institutional platform for collaborative consensus-building, it cannot swallow the fiscal autonomy of states.

---

## 4. Key Doctrines & Fiscal Insights
- **Dual Federalism vs Cooperative Federalism**: GST represents a collaborative federal pool where the Union and States ceded portions of their sovereignty to achieve economic union, but state legislatures retain their democratic voice.
- **Harmonious Federal Taxation**: Emphasized that dispute resolution and fiscal harmonization must proceed through political consensus rather than judicial or executive coercion.
