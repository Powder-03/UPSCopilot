# Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India (2023)

- **Citation**: (2023) 6 SCC 161; 2023 INSC 180
- **Decided On**: March 2, 2023
- **Bench Strength**: 5-Judge Constitution Bench (Unanimous 5-0 decision)
- **Bench Members**: K.M. Joseph, Ajay Rastogi, Aniruddha Bose, Hrishikesh Roy, and C.T. Ravikumar JJ.
- **Constitutional Provisions Interpreted**: Article 324(2), Article 14, Article 19(1)(a), Rule of Law, Free and Fair Elections as Basic Structure

---

## 1. Background & Core Facts
Article 324(2) of the Constitution provides that the appointment of the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and other Election Commissioners (ECs) shall be made by the President, "subject to the provisions of any law made in that behalf by Parliament." For over seven decades since the commencement of the Constitution, Parliament did not enact any law laying down a transparent selection process. Consequently, appointments to this vital constitutional watchdog remained the sole prerogative of the Union Executive (the ruling political party). Public interest petitions were filed challenging this executive monopoly as violative of democracy and institutional independence.

---

## 2. Key Constitutional Questions
1. Does the legislative vacuum under Article 324(2) undermine the independence of the Election Commission of India (ECI)?
2. Is an independent Election Commission an indispensable element of "free and fair elections" and the "Basic Structure"?
3. Can the Supreme Court frame temporary guidelines under Article 142 to ensure institutional independence until Parliament enacts a law?

---

## 3. Majority Ratio Decidendi (Unanimous Ruling)
1. **Free and Fair Elections as Part of Basic Structure**:
   The Court held that **democracy and free and fair elections are foundational features of the Basic Structure**. Democracy can only thrive when the election process is shielded from executive hegemony and ruling-party bias.

2. **The Imperative of Institutional Autonomy**:
   An Election Commission subservient to or beholden to the executive destroys the rule of law. A person who is in a state of obligation to the executive cannot be expected to act independently.

3. **Establishment of a High-Powered Selection Committee**:
   Invoking its powers to fill the legislative vacuum, the Supreme Court directed that the appointment of the CEC and ECs shall be made by the President on the advice of a **three-member High-Powered Committee comprising**:
   1. **The Prime Minister of India**
   2. **The Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha** (or the leader of the single largest opposition party in case there is no recognized LoP)
   3. **The Chief Justice of India**

4. **Rule on Protection of Election Commissioners**:
   The Court called for parity in removal protections between the CEC (who can only be removed via impeachment under Article 324(5)) and the other Election Commissioners, urging Parliament to provide equal constitutional security.

---

## 4. Subsequent Legislative Developments & UPSC Relevance
- Following this ruling, Parliament enacted *The Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023*, which replaced the CJI in the selection committee with a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM.
- This dynamic demonstrates the continuous constitutional dialogue between the Judiciary and the Legislature over institutional appointments and the doctrine of separation of powers.
