# Minerva Mills Ltd. v. Union of India (1980)

- **Citation**: (1980) 3 SCC 625; AIR 1980 SC 1789
- **Decided On**: July 31, 1980
- **Bench Strength**: 5-Judge Constitution Bench
- **Bench Members**: Y.V. Chandrachud C.J., P.N. Bhagwati, V.R. Krishna Iyer, N.L. Untwalia, and P.S. Kailasam JJ.
- **Constitutional Provisions Interpreted**: Article 368(4), Article 368(5), Article 31C, Article 14, Article 19, 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act 1976

---

## 1. Background & Core Facts
Minerva Mills, a textile undertaking in Bangalore, was nationalized by the Central Government under the Sick Textile Undertakings (Taking Over of Management) Act, 1974. The owners challenged the takeover and simultaneously challenged the constitutional validity of Sections 4 and 55 of the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 (enacted during the Emergency). 
- Section 55 had inserted clauses (4) and (5) into Article 368, claiming that amendments made by Parliament could never be questioned in any court and stating there was no limitation whatever on Parliament's amending power.
- Section 4 had expanded Article 31C to give blanket precedence to **all** Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSPs) over Fundamental Rights under Articles 14 and 19.

---

## 2. Key Constitutional Questions
1. Can Parliament amend Article 368 to grant itself unlimited and unreviewable constituent power, effectively extinguishing the Basic Structure Doctrine?
2. Can Directive Principles of State Policy be given absolute supremacy over Fundamental Rights?
3. What is the constitutional relationship between Part III (Fundamental Rights) and Part IV (Directive Principles)?

---

## 3. Majority Ratio Decidendi (4-1 Majority, Chief Justice Y.V. Chandrachud)
1. **Invalidation of Article 368(4) and (5) (Unlimited Amending Power Struck Down)**:
   The Court held that **a limited amending power is itself a basic feature of the Constitution**. Parliament cannot, by exercising its limited power under Article 368, enlarge that very power into an absolute or unlimited power. Clauses (4) and (5) of Article 368 were declared unconstitutional because they abolished judicial review and destroyed the Basic Structure.

2. **Harmonious Balance between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs**:
   The Court struck down the expansion of Article 31C. Chief Justice Chandrachud famously articulated:
   > *"The Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of the balance between Parts III and IV. To give absolute primacy to one over the other is to disturb the harmony of the Constitution. This harmony and balance between fundamental rights and directive principles is an essential feature of the basic structure of the Constitution."*
   
3. **Twin Wheels of the Chariot**:
   Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles are like the two wheels of a chariot—neither is superior to the other. The goals set out in Part IV must be achieved without abrogating the means guaranteed by Part III.

---

## 4. Key Doctrines & Extended Impact
- **Judicial Review as Inviolable**: Reaffirmed that judicial review is an essential element of the Basic Structure that cannot be legislatively or constitutively ousted.
- **Limits on Constituent Power**: Established that Parliament cannot convert its constituent power into a tool of constitutional suicide.
