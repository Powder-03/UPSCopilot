# Nabam Rebia and Bamang Felix v. Deputy Speaker (2016)

- **Citation**: (2016) 8 SCC 1
- **Decided On**: July 13, 2016
- **Bench Strength**: 5-Judge Constitution Bench
- **Bench Members**: J.S. Khehar, Dipak Misra, J. Chelameswar, Madan B. Lokur, and P.C. Ghose JJ.
- **Constitutional Provisions Interpreted**: Article 163, Article 174, Article 179(c), Tenth Schedule, Sixth Schedule

---

## 1. Background & Core Facts
In 2015, political turmoil erupted in Arunachal Pradesh when 21 rebel Congress MLAs rebelled against Chief Minister Nabam Rebia. The Governor (J.P. Rajkhowa) unilaterally advanced the session of the State Legislative Assembly from January 2016 to December 2015 without consulting or receiving the aid and advice of the Chief Minister and Council of Ministers. The Governor also issued a message dictating the agenda of the Assembly to first take up the resolution for the removal of Speaker Nabam Rebia. Consequently, the rebel MLAs met in a community hall, removed the Speaker, and subsequently President's Rule was imposed under Article 356.

---

## 2. Key Constitutional Questions
1. Does the Governor have discretionary power under Article 163 to summon, prorogue, or advance an assembly session under Article 174 without the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers?
2. What are the constitutional boundaries of the Governor’s discretionary powers under Article 163?
3. Can a Speaker facing a notice of removal under Article 179(c) adjudicate disqualification petitions against MLAs under the Tenth Schedule?

---

## 3. Majority Ratio Decidendi
1. **Governor is Bound by Aid and Advice**:
   The Supreme Court held that the **Governor does not possess unfettered discretion under Article 163** to summon, prorogue, or dissolve the Legislative Assembly. Under Article 174, the Governor can summon the House **only on the aid and advice of the Chief Minister and the Council of Ministers**, and not unilaterally.

2. **Strict Demarcation of Discretion under Article 163**:
   The Court ruled that the Governor’s discretionary powers under Article 163(1) are strictly limited to matters where the Constitution expressly requires the Governor to act in his discretion (e.g., Sixth Schedule administration or seeking a floor test when the government has clearly lost its majority). In all other matters, the Governor is a titular constitutional head bound by cabinet advice.

3. **Governor Cannot Act as a Parallel Center of Power**:
   The Governor is not an ombudsman or general supervisor of the state government. The Governor cannot interfere in the internal proceedings or political dynamics of the House.

4. **Restriction on Speaker Facing Removal**:
   The Court held that a Speaker against whom a resolution for removal under Article 179(c) is pending cannot proceed to decide disqualification petitions under the Tenth Schedule against members of the Assembly until the resolution for his removal is disposed of.

5. **Restoration of Government (*Status Quo Ante*)**:
   The Supreme Court ordered the restoration of the dismissed Nabam Rebia government, quashing the Governor’s actions and the subsequent President's Rule.

---

## 4. Key Doctrines & Principles Established
- Reinforced parliamentary democracy at the state level: the real executive power vests with the elected cabinet answerable to the assembly, not the nominated Governor.
- Established strict judicial scrutiny over gubernatorial messages and assembly summoning.

---

- **Polity GS2 Hook**: Essential case to cite whenever evaluating controversies around the "Role of Governor", "Summoning of Assembly", "Misuse of Discretionary Powers (Art 163/174)", and federal friction.
