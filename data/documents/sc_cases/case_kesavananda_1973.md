# Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)

- **Citation**: (1973) 4 SCC 225; AIR 1973 SC 1461
- **Decided On**: April 24, 1973
- **Bench Strength**: 13-Judge Constitution Bench (Largest in Indian Judicial History)
- **Bench Members**: S.M. Sikri C.J., J.M. Shelat, K.S. Hegde, A.N. Grover, B. Jaganmohan Reddy, D.G. Palekar, H.R. Khanna, A.K. Mukherjee, K.K. Mathew, M.H. Beg, S.N. Dwivedi, Y.V. Chandrachud, and A.N. Ray JJ.
- **Constitutional Provisions Interpreted**: Article 368, Article 13, Article 19(1)(f), Article 31, 24th, 25th, and 29th Constitutional Amendments

---

## 1. Background & Core Facts
His Holiness Kesavananda Bharati Sripadagalvaru, head of the Edneer Mutt in Kasaragod, Kerala, challenged the Kerala Land Reforms Amendment Acts of 1969 and 1971 under Article 26 (freedom to manage religious affairs) and Article 14, 19(1)(f), and 31. During the pendency of the petition, Parliament enacted the 24th, 25th, and 29th Constitutional Amendments to circumvent earlier judicial decisions, notably *I.C. Golaknath v. State of Punjab* (1967), which had held that Parliament could not amend Fundamental Rights.

---

## 2. Key Constitutional Questions
1. Does Parliament have unlimited power under Article 368 to amend any provision of the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights?
2. What is the scope and meaning of the word "amendment" under Article 368?
3. Can the constituent power be exercised to abrogate the core identity or foundational structure of the Constitution?

---

## 3. Majority Ratio Decidendi (7-6 Majority)
1. **Basic Structure Doctrine Established**:
   The Supreme Court overruled *Golaknath* and held that Parliament has the power to amend any and every part of the Constitution, including Part III (Fundamental Rights). However, this amending power under Article 368 is not unlimited: **it does not extend to damaging, destroying, or altering the "Basic Structure" or essential identity of the Constitution.**
   
2. **Constituent Power vs Total Destruction**:
   Justice H.R. Khanna delivered the pivotal concurring judgment, observing that the word "amendment" postulates that the old Constitution survives without loss of its identity despite changes in details. Parliament cannot use Article 368 to replace the Constitution with a completely different framework or create a sovereign legislature in place of constitutional supremacy.

3. **Validity of Amendments**:
   - Upheld the 24th Amendment affirming Parliament's constituent power to amend Fundamental Rights.
   - Upheld the first limb of Article 31C (giving primacy to Directive Principles under Art 39(b) and (c) over Art 14 and 19).
   - Struck down the second limb of Article 31C ("...and no law containing a declaration that it is for giving effect to such policy shall be called in question in any court on the ground that it does not give effect to such policy"), holding that judicial review is an inviolable constitutional safeguard.

---

## 4. Recognized Core Features of the Basic Structure
While not exhaustively defined, the judges identified key illustrative pillars:
- Supremacy of the Constitution
- Republican and Democratic form of Government
- Secular character of the Constitution
- Separation of powers between the Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary
- Federal character of the Constitution
- Sovereignty and integrity of India
- Fundamental Rights and Dignity of the Individual
- Power of Judicial Review

---

## 5. UPSC Mains Exam Application & Key Takeaway
- **Key Directive**: Must be cited whenever evaluating Article 368, judicial review vs parliamentary sovereignty, or any constitutional amendment challenging basic rights.
- **Exam Hook**: Acts as an indestructible safety valve preserving constitutionalism against parliamentary majoritarianism and executive authoritarianism.
