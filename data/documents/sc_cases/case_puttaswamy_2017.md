# Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India (2017)

- **Citation**: (2017) 10 SCC 1; AIR 2017 SC 4125
- **Decided On**: August 24, 2017
- **Bench Strength**: 9-Judge Constitution Bench (Unanimous 9-0 decision)
- **Bench Members**: J.S. Khehar C.J., J. Chelameswar, S.A. Bobde, R.K. Agrawal, Rohinton F. Nariman, A.M. Sapre, D.Y. Chandrachud, Sanjay Kishan Kaul, and S. Abdul Nazeer JJ.
- **Constitutional Provisions Interpreted**: Article 21, Article 14, Article 19, Overruled *M.P. Sharma* (1954) and *Kharak Singh* (1962)

---

## 1. Background & Core Facts
Retired High Court Judge K.S. Puttaswamy challenged the constitutional validity of the Aadhaar biometrics scheme, arguing that mandatory collection of fingerprints and iris scans without legislative backing violated the fundamental right to privacy. The Union of India contended that the Indian Constitution does not guarantee a fundamental right to privacy, citing earlier larger-bench judgments in *M.P. Sharma v. Satish Chandra* (1954 - 8 judges) and *Kharak Singh v. State of U.P.* (1962 - 6 judges). A 9-judge Constitution Bench was constituted to determine whether privacy is a constitutionally protected fundamental right.

---

## 2. Key Constitutional Questions
1. Does the Indian Constitution guarantee a fundamental Right to Privacy?
2. If so, which provision of the Constitution protects it?
3. Were *M.P. Sharma* (1954) and *Kharak Singh* (1962) correctly decided on the question of privacy?
4. What are the limits and permissible grounds of state intrusion on personal privacy?

---

## 3. Majority Ratio Decidendi (Unanimous 9-0 Ruling)
1. **Privacy is an Inalienable Fundamental Right**:
   The Court unanimously held that the **Right to Privacy is a fundamental right**, protected as an intrinsic part of the right to life and personal liberty under **Article 21** and as a part of the freedoms guaranteed by Part III of the Constitution.

2. **Overruled Precedents**:
   The Court explicitly overruled *M.P. Sharma* (1954) and *Kharak Singh* (1962) to the extent that they held privacy was not a fundamental right.

3. **Facets of Privacy**:
   - **Bodily Integrity / Autonomy**: Control over one's own body, medical choices, reproductive freedom, and personal dignity.
   - **Informational Privacy**: Control over personal data, digital footprints, and protection against unauthorized surveillance or profiling.
   - **Decisional Autonomy**: Privacy of personal intimacies, marriage, lifestyle choices, and individual decisions free from unwarranted state interference.

4. **The Four-Fold Proportionality Test**:
   Like any fundamental right, privacy is not absolute, but state interference must strictly satisfy the 4-fold proportionality standard:
   1. **Legality**: Existence of an enacted, valid statutory law authorizing the intrusion.
   2. **Legitimate State Aim**: The state action must serve a pressing, legitimate public objective (e.g., national security, crime prevention, public health).
   3. **Suitability (Rational Nexus)**: A rational connection must exist between the measure adopted and the legitimate state objective.
   4. **Necessity (Least Restrictive Measure)**: The measure must be the least intrusive means available, without causing disproportionate harm to the individual.

---

## 4. Key Doctrines & Extended Impacts
- **Dignity Jurisprudence**: Privacy is the bedrock of individual autonomy and human dignity.
- **Catalyst for Rights**: Direct foundation for striking down Section 377 IPC (*Navtej Johar*), decriminalizing adultery (*Joseph Shine*), recognizing abortion rights (*X v. Principal Secretary*), and enacting the *Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023*.

---

## 5. UPSC Mains Exam Application
- **Key Directive**: Must be cited for any question on digital surveillance, DNA databases, Aadhaar, personal data protection, reproductive autonomy, or LGBTQ+ rights.
- **Topper Hook**: Always structure answers testing state digital policies against the 3-fold proportionality test (Legality, Legitimate Aim, Proportionality).
