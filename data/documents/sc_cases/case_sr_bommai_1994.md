# S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)

- **Citation**: (1994) 3 SCC 1; AIR 1994 SC 1918
- **Decided On**: March 11, 1994
- **Bench Strength**: 9-Judge Constitution Bench
- **Bench Members**: S.R. Pandian, A.M. Ahmadi, Kuldip Singh, J.S. Verma, P.B. Sawant, K. Ramaswamy, S.C. Agrawal, Yogeshwar Dayal, and B.P. Jeevan Reddy JJ.
- **Constitutional Provisions Interpreted**: Article 356, Article 355, Article 74(2), Article 163, Basic Structure Doctrine

---

## 1. Background & Core Facts
In 1989, the Janata Dal government led by S.R. Bommai in Karnataka was dismissed by the Governor following defections, without giving the Chief Minister an opportunity to prove his majority on the floor of the Legislative Assembly. Simultaneously, following the demolition of the Babri Masjid in December 1992, the President dismissed BJP-led state governments in Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, and Himachal Pradesh on grounds of threats to secularism and public order.

---

## 2. Key Constitutional Questions
1. Is the presidential proclamation imposing President's Rule under Article 356 subject to judicial review?
2. What is the scope of "satisfaction of the President" under Article 356(1)?
3. Does Article 74(2) bar courts from examining the material basis on which the Council of Ministers advised the President?
4. Are Federalism and Secularism part of the Basic Structure of the Indian Constitution?
5. Where should a Chief Minister's majority be tested—in Raj Bhavan or on the floor of the Assembly?

---

## 3. Majority Ratio Decidendi
1. **Federalism and Secularism are Basic Structure Pillars**:
   The 9-judge bench authoritatively held that both **Federalism** and **Secularism** are essential and inviolable features of the Basic Structure. Any state government acting against secularism can be subjected to action under Article 356.

2. **Judicial Review of Article 356**:
   A proclamation under Article 356 is **not immune from judicial review**. The courts can examine whether the material on which the President formed satisfaction was relevant, whether there was malafide intent, or whether the exercise of power was based on extraneous considerations.

3. **Floor Test is Mandatory**:
   The floor of the Legislative Assembly is the **only legitimate constitutional forum** to test whether a government commands a majority. A Governor’s subjective assessment or "headcount" at Raj Bhavan is impermissible.

4. **Remedy of Restoration & Status Quo Ante**:
   - The Legislative Assembly should not be dissolved immediately upon issuing an Article 356 proclamation; it can only be suspended. Dissolution is permitted only after both Houses of Parliament approve the proclamation.
   - If the proclamation is struck down by the courts as unconstitutional, the court has the power to restore the dismissed government and revive the dissolved assembly.

---

## 4. Operative Guidelines for Governors and Union Executive
- Warning requirement: The Centre must issue a warning notice under Article 355 before invoking Article 356, except in extraordinary emergencies.
- Floor test must be ordered within the shortest possible timeframe (typically 48 hours).
- Article 356 is an exceptional power and should be treated as a measure of "last resort".

---

## 5. UPSC Mains Exam Application
- **Polity GS2 Hook**: Compulsory citation for any question regarding the office of Governor, Centre-State friction, federal balance, or misuse of emergency powers.
- **Key Impact**: Drastically reduced the arbitrary imposition of President's Rule across India post-1994.
