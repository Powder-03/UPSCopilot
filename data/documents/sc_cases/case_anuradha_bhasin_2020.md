# Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India (2020)

- **Citation**: (2020) 3 SCC 637; 2020 INSC 22
- **Decided On**: January 10, 2020
- **Bench Strength**: 3-Judge Bench
- **Bench Members**: N.V. Ramana, R. Subhash Reddy, and B.R. Gavai JJ.
- **Constitutional Provisions Interpreted**: Article 19(1)(a), Article 19(1)(g), Article 19(2), Article 19(6), Section 144 of CrPC, Temporary Suspension of Telecom Services (Public Emergency or Public Safety) Rules 2017

---

## 1. Background & Core Facts
In August 2019, following the dilution of Article 370 and reorganization of the State of Jammu and Kashmir into two Union Territories, the government imposed total mobile, landline, and internet communications shutdowns and widespread Section 144 CrPC movement restrictions across Jammu and Kashmir. Anuradha Bhasin, executive editor of the *Kashmir Times*, filed a writ petition under Article 32 contending that the communications blackout crippled print media and prevented journalists from reporting, violating freedom of the press under Article 19(1)(a). Ghulam Nabi Azad also filed a petition regarding fundamental rights to livelihood and trade over the internet under Article 19(1)(g).

---

## 2. Key Constitutional Questions
1. Is the freedom of speech and expression and the freedom to practice trade/profession over the internet protected under Article 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g)?
2. Can the state impose indefinite suspensions of internet and telecommunication services under Section 5(2) of the Telegraph Act and Telecom Suspension Rules?
3. What procedural safeguards and judicial standards govern internet shutdown orders?
4. What are the constitutional limitations on the exercise of preventive magisterial powers under Section 144 CrPC?

---

## 3. Ratio Decidendi
1. **Freedom of Internet Expression & Trade Protected under Part III**:
   The Supreme Court authoritatively declared that **the freedom of speech and expression under Article 19(1)(a) and the freedom to carry on any trade, business, or profession under Article 19(1)(g) using the medium of the internet are constitutionally protected fundamental rights**.

2. **Indefinite Internet Shutdowns are Impermissible**:
   The Court ruled that **suspension of internet services cannot be indefinite**. An order suspending internet services must strictly be temporary and must adhere to the principles of necessity and proportionality under Article 19(2) and 19(6).

3. **Mandatory Public Disclosure & Publication of Orders**:
   The Court held that **transparency is an essential prerequisite of rule of law**. The state must publish all suspension orders passed under the Telecom Suspension Rules and Section 144 CrPC so that affected citizens can challenge their validity before a court of law. Secret or uncommunicated orders are unlawful.

4. **Periodic Review by Review Committee**:
   Any suspension order passed must be placed before and periodically reviewed by the statutory Review Committee under Rule 2(6) of the Telecom Suspension Rules within five working days.

5. **Guidelines on Section 144 CrPC**:
   The power under Section 144 CrPC cannot be used as a tool to prevent the legitimate expression of grievance, protest, or exercise of democratic rights. It cannot be used against the public at large unless there is a present, material, and imminent danger of public order breakdown.

---

## 4. Key Doctrines & Principles Established
- **Proportionality in the Digital Age**: Any restriction on internet access must be the least intrusive means to achieve the stated security objective; alternatives (such as selective whitelisting of essential websites) must be explored first.
- **Democratic Dissent Protected**: Section 144 cannot be used to suppress peaceful assembly or democratic opposition.
