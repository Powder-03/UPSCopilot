# Govt. of NCT of Delhi v. Union of India (Services Case, 2023)

- **Citation**: (2023) 9 SCC 1; 2023 INSC 518
- **Decided On**: May 11, 2023
- **Bench Strength**: 5-Judge Constitution Bench (Unanimous 5-0 decision)
- **Bench Members**: D.Y. Chandrachud C.J., M.R. Shah, Krishna Murari, Hima Kohli, and P.S. Narasimha JJ.
- **Constitutional Provisions Interpreted**: Article 239AA, Entry 41 of List II (State List), 69th Constitutional Amendment Act 1991, Principle of Representative Democracy and Federalism

---

## 1. Background & Core Facts
A major constitutional dispute arose between the elected Government of the National Capital Territory of Delhi (GNCTD) and the Lieutenant Governor (LG) representing the Union Government over administrative control over civil servants (IAS, DANICS officers) posted in the Government of NCT Delhi. The Union Government claimed that under Article 239AA, Delhi is a Union Territory over which Parliament and the Union Executive have plenary authority over "Services". The Delhi Government argued that without executive control over civil servants, an elected government cannot implement policies or function effectively.

---

## 2. Key Constitutional Questions
1. Does the legislative and executive competence of the GNCTD extend to "Services" under Entry 41 of the State List (List II)?
2. What is the scope of the Lieutenant Governor's executive powers under Article 239AA?
3. How does the constitutional principle of the "Triple Chain of Accountability" apply to the governance of NCT Delhi?

---

## 3. Majority Ratio Decidendi (Unanimous 5-0 Ruling)
1. **Delhi Government Exercises Control Over Services**:
   The Supreme Court unanimously held that the **elected Government of NCT of Delhi has legislative and executive control over administrative civil services (Entry 41 of List II)**, with the sole exception of the three subjects expressly excluded by Article 239AA(3)(a): **Public Order, Police, and Land**.

2. **The "Triple Chain of Accountability"**:
   Chief Justice D.Y. Chandrachud articulated a cornerstone doctrine of democratic governance:
   - **First Link**: Civil servants are accountable to the Council of Ministers (Cabinet).
   - **Second Link**: The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the elected Legislative Assembly.
   - **Third Link**: The Legislative Assembly is accountable to the citizens of Delhi who elected them.
   - *Impact*: If civil servants are insulated from the control of the elected ministers, the chain of accountability snaps, reducing the elected government to a toothless body and defeating the purpose of representative democracy.

3. **Lieutenant Governor Bound by Cabinet Advice**:
   The Lieutenant Governor is bound by the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers in all matters where the Delhi Assembly has legislative competence (which includes Services). The LG cannot act as an independent parallel administrative authority.

4. **Asymmetric Federalism in India**:
   The Court recognized that Article 239AA creates a model of asymmetric federalism tailored to accommodate the special status of the national capital, balancing national interests with the democratic rights of the local electorate.

---

## 4. Subsequent Legislative Developments & UPSC Relevance
- Shortly after this judgment, the President promulgated *The Government of National Capital Territory of Delhi (Amendment) Ordinance, 2023* (later enacted by Parliament as the GNCTD Amendment Act, 2023), creating the National Capital Civil Services Authority (NCCSA) to transfer control back to the LG.
- This represents a classic case study on executive-judicial friction, parliamentary overrides, and the constitutional status of union territories with assemblies.
