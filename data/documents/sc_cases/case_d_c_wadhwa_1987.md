# D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar (1987)

- **Citation**: (1987) 1 SCC 378; AIR 1987 SC 579
- **Decided On**: December 20, 1986
- **Bench Strength**: 5-Judge Constitution Bench
- **Bench Members**: P.N. Bhagwati C.J., R.S. Pathak, D.P. Madon, S. Murtaza Fazal Ali, and V. Khalid JJ.
- **Constitutional Provisions Interpreted**: Article 213, Article 123, Article 174, Separation of Powers, Colorable Legislation

---

## 1. Background & Core Facts
Dr. D.C. Wadhwa, a professor of economics at the Gokhale Institute of Politics and Economics in Pune, conducted thorough empirical research into the legislative practices of the State of Bihar. He discovered an astonishing constitutional subversion: between 1967 and 1981, the Governor of Bihar promulgated **256 ordinances**, and all of them were kept alive for years (some for over 14 years!) through repeated re-promulgation on the exact same terms immediately after the prorogation of each legislative session, without ever placing the bills before the State Legislature. Dr. Wadhwa filed a public interest writ petition under Article 32 challenging this systemic "Ordinance Raj".

---

## 2. Key Constitutional Questions
1. Does the Executive have the constitutional power under Article 213 (or Article 123 for the President) to repeatedly re-promulgate ordinances without introducing and passing them in the legislature?
2. Does perpetual re-promulgation amount to an abuse of constitutional power and a "fraud on the Constitution"?
3. Does a citizen/academic researcher have locus standi under Article 32 to challenge systemic unconstitutional executive practices?

---

## 3. Majority Ratio Decidendi (Unanimous 5-0 Ruling, Chief Justice P.N. Bhagwati)
1. **Re-promulgation is a Fraud on the Constitution**:
   The Supreme Court struck down the practice of re-promulgating ordinances as **unconstitutional, a colorable exercise of power, and a subversion of the democratic legislative process**.
   > *"The executive cannot usurp the legislative function of the legislature by repeatedly re-promulgating ordinances without submitting them to the assembly. Such an exercise of power is a subversion of the democratic process and a fraud on the Constitution."*

2. **Emergency Exception vs Normal Rule**:
   The power to make ordinances under Article 123 and Article 213 is an **extraordinary emergency power** granted to the executive to deal with urgent, unforeseen situations when the legislature is not in session. It cannot be converted into an everyday substitute for the primary law-making power of the elected legislature.

3. **Mandatory Legislative Scrutiny**:
   An ordinance must be laid before the Legislative Assembly as soon as it reconvenes and will cease to operate at the expiration of 6 weeks from reassembly. Allowing the executive to bypass the legislature and govern purely by decree destroys the core principle of constitutional separation of powers.

4. **Locus Standi Recognized**:
   The Court recognized that every citizen has a vital interest in ensuring that the governance of the state is conducted according to the Constitution and rule of law, affirming Dr. Wadhwa's PIL standing.

---

## 4. Subsequent Re-affirmation in *Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar* (2017)
In 2017, a 7-judge Constitution Bench in *Krishna Kumar Singh* re-examined and fortified *D.C. Wadhwa*, holding that:
- Placing an ordinance before the legislature is a mandatory constitutional requirement.
- Re-promulgation is unconstitutional per se.
- The satisfaction of the President/Governor under Articles 123 and 213 is subject to judicial review.
