# Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013)

- **Citation**: (2013) 7 SCC 653; AIR 2013 SC 2662
- **Decided On**: July 10, 2013
- **Bench Strength**: 2-Judge Bench
- **Bench Members**: A.K. Patnaik and S.J. Mukhopadhaya JJ.
- **Constitutional Provisions Interpreted**: Article 102(1)(e), Article 191(1)(e), Section 8(4) of the Representation of the People Act, 1951

---

## 1. Background & Core Facts
Section 8(1), (2), and (3) of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 (RPA) sets out disqualifications for contesting elections or sitting as an MP/MLA upon conviction for specified criminal offences or upon being sentenced to imprisonment for not less than two years. However, **Section 8(4)** provided a carve-out: if a sitting member of Parliament or State Legislature was convicted, the disqualification would not take effect for a period of three months, or until an appeal or revision petition filed within those three months was disposed of by a higher court. Advocate Lily Thomas challenged Section 8(4) as ultra vires the Constitution.

---

## 2. Key Constitutional Questions
1. Did Parliament have the legislative competence under Articles 102(1)(e) and 191(1)(e) to create a differential disqualification rule for sitting legislators compared to non-sitting candidates?
2. Does Section 8(4) violate Article 14 by creating an artificial and protective immunity for convicted sitting politicians?

---

## 3. Ratio Decidendi
1. **Section 8(4) of RPA 1951 Struck Down as Unconstitutional**:
   The Supreme Court held that **Section 8(4) of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 was ultra vires the Constitution**. Parliament lacks the constitutional competence under Articles 102(1)(e) and 191(1)(e) to defer the disqualification of sitting members.

2. **No Differential Standard for Sitting vs Aspiring Legislators**:
   Articles 102(1)(e) and 191(1)(e) empower Parliament to lay down the same disqualifications for being chosen as, and for being, a member of either House. The Constitution mandates an identical disqualification standard:
   - A sitting MP/MLA convicted of a disqualifying offence cannot be shielded by a special 3-month moratorium that ordinary citizens and candidate hopefuls do not enjoy.

3. **Immediate Effect of Disqualification**:
   The Court ruled that the moment a sitting MP or MLA is convicted of a specified offence and sentenced to 2 or more years of imprisonment, **their seat automatically falls vacant immediately upon the pronouncement of judgment**.

4. **Exception - Suspension of Conviction**:
   Disqualification is lifted only if an appellate court specifically **stays or suspends the conviction itself** under Section 389(1) of the CrPC, and not merely grants bail or suspends the execution of the sentence.

---

## 4. Key Doctrines & Democratic Impact
- Landmark milestone in combating the **criminalization of politics** in India.
- Ended the practice of convicted lawmakers continuing in office for decades during the pendency of slow-moving appeals.

---
