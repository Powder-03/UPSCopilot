# NCERT Class 12: Introductory Macroeconomics (Core Conceptual Extract)

## 1. National Income Accounting

### Core Aggregates & Measurement
- **Gross Domestic Product (GDP)**: The total monetary/market value of all final goods and services produced within the geographic domestic territory of a country during a specified financial year.
  - Excludes intermediate goods to prevent **double counting**.
  - Excludes transfer payments (pensions, scholarships), illegal activities, and unpaid domestic care work.
- **Gross National Product (GNP)**: Total value of goods and services produced by the normal residents of a country, regardless of geographical location.
  $$\text{GNP} = \text{GDP} + \text{Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)}$$
  $$\text{NFIA} = \text{Factor income earned by residents abroad} - \text{Factor income earned by non-residents in domestic territory}$$
- **Net Aggregates (Depreciation Adjustment)**:
  - **Net Domestic Product (NDP)** = $\text{GDP} - \text{Depreciation (Consumption of Fixed Capital)}$.
  - **Net National Product (NNP) at Factor Cost**: Recognized as the official **National Income**.
    $$\text{NNP}_{\text{FC}} = \text{NNP}_{\text{MP}} - \text{Net Indirect Taxes (Indirect Taxes} - \text{Subsidies)}$$
- **Gross Value Added (GVA)**:
  - Measures the contribution of each individual producing sector/enterprise to the economy by deducting intermediate consumption from gross output.
  - $\text{GVA at Basic Prices} = \text{GVA at Factor Cost} + \text{Production Taxes} - \text{Production Subsidies}$.
  - $\text{GDP at Market Prices} = \text{GVA at Basic Prices} + \text{Product Taxes} - \text{Product Subsidies}$.

### Nominal GDP, Real GDP & The GDP Deflator
- **Nominal GDP**: GDP evaluated at current market prices of the year in which output is produced. Influenced by both physical volume changes and price inflation.
- **Real GDP**: GDP evaluated at constant base-year prices (currently base year 2011-12 in India). Isolates pure volume/output expansion from price distortions; true indicator of economic growth.
- **GDP Deflator**: The ratio of Nominal GDP to Real GDP:
  $$\text{GDP Deflator} = \left(\frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{Real GDP}}\right) \times 100$$
  - Reflects the price inflation of all domestically produced goods and services in the economy (broader than Consumer Price Index - CPI or Wholesale Price Index - WPI).

## 2. Money, Banking & Monetary Transmission

### Money Supply Aggregates (RBI Definitions)
- **$M_1$ (Narrow Money - Most Liquid)**:
  $$M_1 = \text{Currency with Public (Notes + Coins)} + \text{Demand Deposits with Banking System} + \text{'Other' Deposits with RBI}$$
- **$M_2$**: $M_1 + \text{Savings deposits with Post Office Savings Banks}$.
- **$M_3$ (Broad Money - Standard Benchmark for Monetary Policy)**:
  $$M_3 = M_1 + \text{Time Deposits with the Banking System (Fixed & Recurring Deposits)}$$
- **$M_4$**: $M_3 + \text{Total Deposits with Post Office Savings Organisation (excluding National Savings Certificates - NSC)}$.
- **$M_0$ (Reserve Money / High-Powered Money / Monetary Base)**:
  $$M_0 = \text{Currency in Circulation} + \text{Bankers' Deposits with RBI} + \text{'Other' Deposits with RBI}$$
  - The liability base created directly by the Central Bank upon which commercial banks expand credit.

### The Money Multiplier Mechanism
- **Money Multiplier ($m$)**: The ratio of Broad Money ($M_3$) to High-Powered Money ($M_0$):
  $$m = \frac{M_3}{M_0} = \frac{1 + c}{c + r}$$
  where $c = \frac{\text{Currency held by public}}{\text{Demand/Time deposits}}$ (Currency-Deposit Ratio) and $r = \text{Reserve-Deposit Ratio (CRR + SLR)}$.
- An increase in digital banking and financial inclusion (UPI, Jan Dhan) lowers $c$, expanding the money multiplier and increasing credit creation.

### Quantitative Monetary Policy Instruments
1. **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)**: The percentage of a commercial bank's Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) that it must maintain as liquid cash balances with the RBI. No interest is paid by the RBI on CRR balances.
2. **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)**: The percentage of NDTL that banks must maintain in safe, liquid unencumbered assets (primarily Central & State Government Securities - G-Secs, Gold, Cash).
3. **Repo Rate**: The policy interest rate at which the RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks against collateral of government securities under the Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF).
4. **Standing Deposit Facility (SDF)**: Introduced in 2022; collateral-free liquidity absorption tool operating 25 bps below the Repo Rate.
5. **Open Market Operations (OMO)**: Outright purchase or sale of government securities in the open market by the RBI to inject or absorb permanent durable liquidity.

## 3. Government Budget & Deficit Indicators

### Budgetary Receipts & Expenditures Anatomy
- **Revenue Budget**:
  - **Revenue Receipts**: Receipts that neither create a liability nor cause any reduction in government assets.
    - *Tax Revenue*: Direct taxes (Income tax, Corporate tax) and Indirect taxes (GST, Customs, Central Excise).
    - *Non-Tax Revenue*: Interest receipts on loans given to states, dividends and profits from PSUs/RBI, fees, user charges.
  - **Revenue Expenditure**: Expenditure incurred for the normal running of government services and administrative departments that neither creates physical/financial assets nor reduces debt liabilities (e.g., interest payments on accumulated debt, food/fertilizer subsidies, defense salaries and pensions, administrative expenses).
- **Capital Budget**:
  - **Capital Receipts**: Receipts that either create a debt liability or cause a reduction in government assets.
    - *Debt Capital Receipts*: Market borrowings, Treasury bills, external commercial debt.
    - *Non-Debt Capital Receipts*: Recovery of loans and advances, disinvestment/monetization proceeds of public sector assets.
  - **Capital Expenditure (Capex)**: Outlays resulting in the creation of physical infrastructure (roads, railways, power plants) or acquisition of financial assets, and reduction of liabilities (repayment of borrowings). Capex carries a high **fiscal multiplier** ($\sim 2.5 - 3.0$), stimulating long-term private investment ("crowding-in" effect).

### Four Key Deficit Concepts
1. **Revenue Deficit (RD)**:
   $$\text{RD} = \text{Revenue Expenditure} - \text{Revenue Receipts}$$
   Indicates the government is dis-saving and borrowing to fund consumption expenditures.
2. **Effective Revenue Deficit (ERD)**:
   $$\text{ERD} = \text{Revenue Deficit} - \text{Grants given to States for creation of Capital Assets}$$
3. **Fiscal Deficit (FD)**:
   $$\text{FD} = \text{Total Budgetary Expenditure} - (\text{Revenue Receipts} + \text{Non-Debt Capital Receipts})$$
   - Represents the **total gross borrowing requirement** of the Government from all sources (market, RBI, external) during the year.
4. **Primary Deficit (PD)**:
   $$\text{PD} = \text{Fiscal Deficit} - \text{Interest Payments on Past Debt}$$
   - Shows the borrowing required to meet current year's fiscal imbalance, isolating the legacy burden of past debt accumulation.

## 4. External Sector & Balance of Payments (BoP)

### Structure of the Balance of Payments Account
A systematic double-entry accounting record of all economic and financial transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world during a financial year.

$$\text{Balance of Payments} = \text{Current Account} + \text{Capital Account} + \text{Errors \& Omissions} = \Delta \text{Foreign Exchange Reserves}$$

### 1. Current Account
Records transactions involving export/import of goods, services, and unilateral transfers that do not alter the asset-liability position of the nation:
- **Merchandise Trade Balance**: $\text{Merchandise Exports} - \text{Merchandise Imports}$ (India runs a chronic structural merchandise deficit driven by crude oil, gold, electronics).
- **Net Invisibles**:
  - *Services Trade*: Software/IT services, business process management, travel, transportation (India runs a massive structural services surplus).
  - *Transfers / Remittances*: Private remittances from Indian diaspora (India is the world's largest recipient of inward remittances, exceeding \$120 billion annually), grants, gifts.
  - *Income*: Investment income (dividends, interest payments, profits).
- **Current Account Deficit (CAD)**: Occurs when total imports of goods, services, and transfers exceed total exports. Sustainable threshold for India is typically around **2.0% to 2.5% of GDP**.

### 2. Capital Account
Records all international transactions involving the acquisition or disposal of financial and physical assets:
- **Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)**: Stable, long-term capital inflow involving ownership stake ($\ge 10\%$) and management control in domestic enterprises; non-debt creating.
- **Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI)**: Volatile, short-term "hot money" invested in listed equities and debt securities; sensitive to US Federal Reserve interest rate cycles.
- **External Commercial Borrowings (ECB)**: Commercial loans raised by Indian corporate entities from non-resident lenders.
- **NRI Deposits**: Foreign currency deposits maintained by non-resident Indians in domestic banks (e.g., FCNR(B), NRE accounts).

### Foreign Exchange Rate Dynamics
- **Nominal Effective Exchange Rate (NEER)**: Trade-weighted unadjusted average of bilateral exchange rates of the Rupee against a basket of currencies of major trading partners (40-currency basket).
- **Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER)**: NEER adjusted for inflation differentials between India and its trading partners. A REER above 100 indicates real overvaluation of the Rupee (reducing export price competitiveness).
- India operates a **Managed Floating Exchange Rate System** (LERS / Market-determined rate since 1993), where the RBI intervenes in the spot and forward forex markets solely to curb speculative volatility, without targeting any fixed numerical exchange rate band.
