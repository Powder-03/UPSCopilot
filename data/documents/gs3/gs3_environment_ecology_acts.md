# GS-3: Environment, Ecology, Statutory Acts & Climate Architecture (Canonical Dossier)

## 1. Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 & The 2022 Landmark Amendment

### Core Statutory Framework
- Enacted under Article 252 (and later anchored under Entry 17B of the Concurrent List via the 42nd Amendment).
- **Section 9 - Prohibition of Hunting**: Absolute statutory ban on hunting of any wild animal specified in Schedules I and II, except in self-defense, or when declared diseased/dangerous to human life by the Chief Wildlife Warden (Sections 11 and 12).
- **Protected Area Network**:
  - **Sanctuaries (Section 18–26)**: Certain rights of people (e.g., grazing of livestock) may be permitted by the District Collector and Chief Wildlife Warden.
  - **National Parks (Section 35)**: Highest statutory protection; **zero human exploitation or grazing permitted**; boundaries can only be altered on the recommendation of the National Board for Wildlife (NBWL).
  - **Conservation Reserves & Community Reserves (Section 36A–36D)**: Buffer corridors between national parks, managed collaboratively with local communities.

### The Wildlife (Protection) Amendment Act, 2022
- **CITES Integration**: Formally incorporates India's obligations under the **Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora (CITES)**.
- **Rationalization of Schedules (Reduced from 6 down to 4)**:
  1. **Schedule I**: Highest level of statutory protection for endangered animals (e.g., Tiger, Asiatic Lion, Snow Leopard, Great Indian Bustard, Gangetic Dolphin). Maximum penalties for violations.
  2. **Schedule II**: Animals requiring lesser protection compared to Schedule I.
  3. **Schedule III**: Protected plant species (e.g., Red Vanda, Blue Vanda, Pitcher plant, Ladies Slipper Orchid).
  4. **Schedule IV**: **CITES Species**: Enforces trade regulation and export/import permitting for specimens listed under CITES Appendices I, II, and III.
- **Abolition of Schedule V (Vermin)**:
  - The previous Schedule V (which permitted hunting of common crows, fruit bats, mice, and rats) was **completely deleted**. The Central Government must now formally notify any wild animal as vermin for a specified area and period.
- **Captive Animals & Live Elephants (Section 43 Amendment)**:
  - Permits the transfer or transport of a live captive elephant for religious or any other purpose, subject to terms prescribed by the Central Government.
- **Institutional Bodies**:
  - **National Board for Wildlife (NBWL)**: Chaired by the Prime Minister (apex policy body).
  - **National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA, Section 38L)**: Statutory body constituted under the 2006 amendment; oversees Project Tiger, approves Tiger Conservation Plans (TCPs), and notifies core and buffer zones of Tiger Reserves.
  - **Central Zoo Authority (CZA, Section 38A)**: Regulates, accredits, and oversees captive breeding and conservation standards across all Indian zoos.

## 2. Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 (EPA)

### Legislative Origin & Umbrella Framework
- Enacted under **Article 253** of the Constitution (legislation to give effect to international agreements) in the wake of the catastrophic **Bhopal Gas Tragedy (1984)** and the United Nations Conference on the Human Environment (Stockholm, 1972).
- Operates as an **umbrella legislation** coordinating the activities of various central and state authorities under the Water Act 1974 and Air Act 1981.
- **Section 3 - Central Government Powers**:
  - Broad, comprehensive authority to take all measures for protecting and improving the quality of the environment and preventing, controlling, and abating environmental pollution.
  - Authority to lay down standards for emissions, discharge of environmental pollutants, and industrial location restrictions (e.g., Coastal Regulation Zone - CRZ notifications).

### Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA Notification, 2006)
- Issued under Section 3 of the EPA 1986.
- Mandates prior environmental clearance for 39 broad categories of developmental projects (mining, thermal power, highways, ports).
- **Four-Stage Clearance Process**:
  1. **Screening**: Categorizes projects into:
     - *Category A*: Large-scale projects with high potential impact; evaluated by the Expert Appraisal Committee (EAC) and cleared at the Central Level by MoEFCC.
     - *Category B*: Evaluated at the State level by the State Expert Appraisal Committee (SEAC) and cleared by the State Environment Impact Assessment Authority (SEIAA).
  2. **Scoping**: Formulates comprehensive Terms of Reference (ToR) for preparing the draft EIA report.
  3. **Public Consultation**: Mandatory public hearing conducted by the State Pollution Control Board to ascertain local community objections and environmental concerns.
  4. **Appraisal**: Final technical evaluation of the EIA report and public hearing proceedings by the EAC/SEAC.

## 3. Biological Diversity Act, 2002 & 2023 Amendment

### Three-Tier Institutional Architecture
Enacted to give effect to the **United Nations Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD, 1992)**:
1. **National Biodiversity Authority (NBA, Chennai)**: Apex statutory body; regulates access to biological resources by foreign entities, commercial utilization, and intellectual property claims.
2. **State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs)**: Regulate domestic commercial utilization and advise state governments.
3. **Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)**: Constituted at the local Gram Panchayat and Municipality level; responsible for promoting conservation, sustainable use, and documentation of local biological heritage.
   - **People's Biodiversity Registers (PBRs)**: Comprehensive statutory documentation of local traditional biological knowledge, flora, fauna, and indigenous practices.

### Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) & Nagoya Protocol
- Foreign and domestic commercial entities utilizing Indian biological resources or associated traditional knowledge must share a portion of the financial proceeds (0.1% to 0.5% of annual turnover) with the local communities and BMCs who conserved those resources.
- **Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023**:
  - Decriminalizes offences and replaces imprisonment with civil monetary penalties.
  - Exempts registered **AYUSH practitioners** (Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, Homeopathy) and local folk healers from giving prior intimation to SBBs for accessing biological resources.
  - Fast-tracks patent application procedures for Indian entities.

## 4. International Climate Commitments & National Targets

### India's "Panchamrit" Targets (UNFCCC COP26 Glasgow & COP28 Dubai)
Presented by Prime Minister Narendra Modi as India's upgraded Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs):
1. **500 GW Non-Fossil Energy Capacity**: Reach 500 GW of cumulative electric power installed capacity from non-fossil fuel sources by 2030.
2. **50% Renewable Energy**: Meet 50% of cumulative electric power installed capacity from renewable energy sources by 2030 (achieved over 44% by 2024).
3. **1 Billion Tonne Carbon Reduction**: Reduce total projected carbon emissions by one billion tonnes from now until 2030.
4. **45% Carbon Intensity Reduction**: Reduce emissions intensity of its GDP by **45% by 2030**, compared to the 2005 baseline level.
5. **Net Zero Greenhouse Gas Emissions by 2070**: Ultimate long-term decarbonization commitment.

### Mission LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment)
- Global mass movement launched at COP26 and institutionalized globally during India's G20 Presidency.
- Shifts the climate narrative from supply-side macro-policies to **demand-side individual and community behavioral nudges** (circular economy, energy conservation, elimination of single-use plastics, sustainable dietary choices).

## 5. National Green Tribunal Act, 2010 (NGT)

### Judicial Mandate & Jurisdiction
- Constituted under **Article 21** (Right to a healthy environment as an integral component of the Right to Life) and the NGT Act 2010.
- Makes India the third country globally (after Australia and New Zealand) to establish a specialized environmental court.
- **Composition**: Full-time Judicial Chairperson (retired Supreme Court judge or High Court Chief Justice), Judicial Members, and Expert Members with scientific credentials.
- **Guiding Principles**:
  - Bound by the principles of **Natural Justice** (not bound by the rigid procedural rules of the Code of Civil Procedure 1908 or Indian Evidence Act 1872).
  - Explicitly applies the **Precautionary Principle**, the **Polluter Pays Principle**, and the principle of **Sustainable Development**.
  - Statutory mandate to endeavor for disposal of environmental applications/appeals within **6 months** of filing.
- **Scheduled Acts Within NGT Jurisdiction (7 Statutes)**:
  1. Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974.
  2. Water Cess Act, 1977.
  3. Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981.
  4. Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.
  5. Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991.
  6. Biological Diversity Act, 2002.
  7. Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980.
  *(Note: The Wildlife Protection Act 1972 and the Indian Forest Act 1927 are explicitly excluded from NGT jurisdiction; appeals lie directly to High Courts/Supreme Court).*
