# GS-3: Science & Technology, Space Policy & Emerging Frontiers (Canonical Dossier)

## 1. Indian Space Policy 2023 & Landmark Missions

### The Tripartite Institutional Architecture
Approved by the Union Cabinet in April 2023, formally unbundling the commercial and regulatory functions of India's space ecosystem:
1. **Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)**:
   - Departs from routine commercial manufacturing and satellite operation.
   - Refocuses exclusively on **advanced research and development**, deep-space exploration missions, cutting-edge aerospace engineering, and national security space applications.
2. **Indian National Space Promotion and Authorization Centre (IN-SPACe)**:
   - Operates as an **autonomous, single-window nodal agency** housed within the Department of Space.
   - Authorizes, promotes, hand-holds, and regulates space activities of Non-Government Entities (NGEs / private space startups).
   - Grants private players non-discriminatory access to ISRO facilities, test launchpads, and geospatial data.
3. **NewSpace India Limited (NSIL)**:
   - Central Public Sector Enterprise operating as the **sole commercial arm** of the Department of Space.
   - Commercializes space technologies developed by ISRO, produces the Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV) and Small Satellite Launch Vehicle (SSLV) through Indian industry consortia, and markets commercial launch services globally.

### Landmark Space Exploration Missions
- **Chandrayaan-3 (August 2023)**:
  - India became the **first country to land near the lunar south pole** and the fourth nation globally to achieve a soft lunar landing.
  - Landing site designated **"Shiv Shakti Point"**; Vikram lander and Pragyan rover conducted in-situ experiments confirming the presence of Sulphur, Iron, Aluminium, and measuring lunar surface thermal conductivity (ChaSTE experiment) and plasma density (RAMBHA).
- **Aditya-L1 (September 2023)**:
  - India's first dedicated solar observatory spacecraft placed in a **Halo Orbit around the Sun-Earth Lagrangian Point 1 (L1)**, located ~1.5 million km from Earth.
  - Uninterrupted, continuous view of the Sun without eclipses; carries 7 scientific payloads studying the solar corona, chromospheric dynamics, coronal mass ejections (CMEs), and solar wind flares.
- **Gaganyaan Programme**:
  - India's indigenous Human Spaceflight Programme designed to demonstrate human spaceflight capability to Low Earth Orbit (LEO, 400 km) with a 3-member crew for up to 3 days, returning safely to Indian waters.
  - Carries female-looking humanoid robot **"Vyommitra"** for pre-crew flight testing; uses human-rated LVM3 (HLVM3) launch vehicle with integrated Crew Escape System (CES).
- **NISAR (NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar)**:
  - First satellite mission to utilize dual frequencies (L-band from NASA and S-band from ISRO) in a SweepSAR configuration to map global land and ice surface deformation with millimeter-scale accuracy, tracking ecosystem disturbances, ice-sheet collapses, and earthquakes.

## 2. Emerging Deep Technologies: Quantum & Artificial Intelligence

### National Quantum Mission (NQM, 2023–2031)
- Approved by the Union Cabinet with an outlay of **₹6,003 crore** over 8 years, positioning India among the top 6 nations pioneering quantum technology.
- **Four Thematic Hubs (T-Hubs)**:
  1. **Quantum Computing**: Developing intermediate-scale quantum computers with **50 to 1,000 physical qubits** in 8 years on platforms like superconducting and trapped ion systems.
  2. **Quantum Communication**: Satellite-based secure quantum communications between ground stations over 2,000 km, inter-city quantum key distribution (QKD), and multi-node quantum networks.
  3. **Quantum Sensing & Metrology**: High-sensitivity magnetometers, atomic clocks for precision navigation, and quantum gravimeters.
  4. **Quantum Materials & Devices**: Fabrication of topological insulators, 2D quantum materials, and superconducting qubits.

### IndiaAI Mission (2024)
- Flagship initiative approved with a budget of **₹10,372 crore** under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY).
- **Seven Core Pillars**:
  1. **IndiaAI Compute Capacity**: Government-subsidized public-private partnership establishing a centralized computing infrastructure of **10,000 or more Graphical Processing Units (GPUs)** accessible to AI startups and academic researchers.
  2. **IndiaAI Innovation Centre**: Developing sovereign indigenous foundational Large Language Models (LLMs) and multimodal models trained on diverse Indian linguistic datasets.
  3. **IndiaAI Datasets Platform**: Single-point national data repository providing high-quality, sanitized non-personal public sector datasets to domestic AI developers.
  4. **FutureSkills**: Training 30,000 young professionals in foundational AI engineering and prompt design.
  5. **Safe & Trusted AI**: Formulating governance frameworks, ethical algorithms, and anti-deepfake watermarking standards.
- **Bhashini (National Language Translation Mission)**:
  - AI-driven open-source language translation ecosystem breaking linguistic barriers across all **22 Scheduled Indian Languages**, powering cross-lingual public service delivery.

## 3. The India Semiconductor Mission (ISM) & Electronics Manufacturing

### Strategic Context & Global Supply Chain Resilience
- Chips/semiconductors represent the "new oil" of the modern economy; geopolitical tensions in the Taiwan Strait (Taiwan produces $>60\%$ of world's semiconductors and $>90\%$ of advanced sub-10nm nodes) necessitate strategic domestic supply chain diversification.
- **Semicon India Programme (₹76,000 Crore Financial Outlay)**:
  - Provides uniform fiscal support of **50% of the total project cost on a pari-passu basis** for:
    - *Silicon Semiconductor Fabs*: Commercial wafer fabrication plants.
    - *Display Fabs*: AMOLED and TFT LCD displays.
    - *Compound Semiconductors, Silicon Photonics, and Sensors*.
    - *Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test (OSAT) / ATMP Facilities*: Assembly, testing, marking, and packaging of raw silicon wafers into finished microchips.
  - Major approved projects: Tata Electronics Semiconductor Fab in Dholera (Gujarat) in partnership with Taiwan's PSMC; Micron's OSAT facility in Sanand (Gujarat).

## 4. Biotechnology & Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)

### Genetically Modified (GM) Crops & Regulatory Architecture
- Governed under the *Rules for the Manufacture, Use, Import, Export and Storage of Hazardous Microorganisms, Genetically Engineered Organisms or Cells, 1989* issued under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.
- **Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee (GEAC)**: Apex statutory body housed in MoEFCC responsible for approving proposals relating to the commercial release and environmental trials of GM organisms.
- **Current Landscape**:
  - *Bt Cotton (2002)*: Only GM crop approved for commercial cultivation in India. Incorporates *Cry1Ac* and *Cry2Ab* genes from *Bacillus thuringiensis*, conferring resistance to the American Bollworm.
  - *GM Mustard (DMH-11)*: Developed by Centre for Genetic Manipulation of Crop Plants (Delhi University) using Barnase-Barstar system for high-yielding hybrid mustard seeds; approved for environmental release by GEAC in 2022 (presently subject to Supreme Court review).

### Section 3(d) of the Indian Patents Act, 1970 & Anti-Evergreening
- **Statutory Provision (Section 3(d))**:
  - Precludes from patentability the *"mere discovery of a new form of a known substance which does not result in the enhancement of the known efficacy of that substance"*.
  - Enacted to prevent **"Evergreening"** — a pharmaceutical tactic where drug monopolies make trivial chemical modifications (isomers, salts, polymorphs) to existing expiring patents to extend monopoly pricing.
- **Novartis v. Union of India (2013 - Supreme Court Landmark)**:
  - Swiss pharma giant Novartis sought a patent for the beta-crystalline form of *Imatinib Mesylate* (cancer drug **Glivec**).
  - Supreme Court ruled that "efficacy" under Section 3(d) strictly means **therapeutic efficacy** (healing capability on the human body), and not merely physical stability or bio-availability improvements. The patent was rejected, safeguarding affordable generic oncology medication access across the developing world.
- **Compulsory Licensing (Section 84)**:
  - Authorizes the Controller General of Patents to grant a compulsory license to a domestic generic manufacturer if the reasonable requirements of the public with respect to the patented invention have not been satisfied, or if it is not available at a reasonably affordable price (*Natco Pharma v. Bayer, 2012 - Nexavar kidney cancer drug*).
