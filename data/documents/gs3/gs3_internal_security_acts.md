# GS-3: Internal Security, Counter-Terrorism Legislation & Border Defense (Canonical Dossier)

## 1. Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967 (UAPA) & The 2019 Amendment

### Legislative Architecture & Definitions
- Enacted under the reasonable restrictions clause of **Article 19(2)** (sovereignty and integrity of India).
- **Section 2(o) - "Unlawful Activity"**: Any action taken by an individual or association (by words spoken or written, or by signs or visible representation) which disclaims, questions, disrupts, or is intended to disrupt the sovereignty and territorial integrity of India, or which causes or is intended to cause disaffection against India.
- **Section 15 - "Terrorist Act"**: Any act done with intent to threaten or likely to threaten the unity, integrity, security, economic security, or sovereignty of India, or with intent to strike terror in the people or any section of the people in India or in any foreign country, by using bombs, dynamite, firearms, lethal weapons, poisonous gases, or nuclear/biological substances.

### The Landmark 2019 Amendment
- **Individual Designation (Section 35 Amendment)**:
  - Previously, the Central Government could only designate **organizations** as terrorist organizations by adding them to the First Schedule.
  - The 2019 Amendment empowered the Central Government to **designate individual persons as terrorists** and add their names to the **Fourth Schedule** without prior judicial hearing.
  - Review Mechanism: An aggrieved person can apply to the Central Government for de-notification; if rejected, they can appeal to a Review Committee chaired by a retired High Court judge (Section 36).
- **Expanded Powers for NIA (Section 25 & 43)**:
  - Authorizes the Director General of the National Investigation Agency (NIA) to approve the seizure or attachment of property representing proceeds of terrorism (removing the requirement of seeking prior permission from the State Director General of Police).
  - Empowers NIA officers of the rank of **Inspector and above** to investigate UAPA cases (previously restricted to DSP or Assistant Commissioner rank).

### Stringent Bail Regime: The Watali vs Najeeb Jurisprudence
- **Section 43D(5) - Inverted Bail Burden**:
  - Mandates that no person accused of an offence under Chapters IV (terrorist acts) and VI (terrorist organizations) shall be released on bail if the Court, on a perusal of the case diary or police report, is of the opinion that there are **reasonable grounds for believing that the accusation against such person is *prima facie* true**.
- **NIA v. Zahoor Ahmad Shah Watali (2019 - Supreme Court)**:
  - Ruled that at the bail stage, courts **cannot conduct a mini-trial** or scrutinize the probative value or reliability of prosecution evidence. The court must accept the prosecution documents at face value, making regular bail nearly impossible to obtain.
- **Union of India v. K.A. Najeeb (2021 - Supreme Court)**:
  - Created a vital constitutional exception: Ruled that statutory restrictions on bail under Section 43D(5) **do not oust the jurisdiction of constitutional courts (High Courts and Supreme Court) to grant bail** when there is an egregious violation of the accused's Fundamental Right to a **Speedy Trial under Article 21** due to prolonged, indefinite incarceration without trial.

## 2. National Investigation Agency Act, 2008 (NIA Act) & 2019 Expansion

### Legislative Genesis & Mandate
- Enacted in direct response to the **26/11 Mumbai Terror Attacks (2008)** to create a specialized, central federal investigative agency.
- **Concurrent & Overriding Federal Jurisdiction (Section 6)**:
  - Once a scheduled offence is referred by a state government or directed *suo motu* by the Central Government, the NIA takes over investigation, and state police officers are statutorily barred from proceeding further.

### The 2019 Amendment Act: Broadening the Security Horizon
1. **Extra-Territorial Jurisdiction (Section 1(2) & 7A)**:
   - Empowers the NIA to investigate scheduled offences committed **outside the territory of India** against Indian citizens or affecting the interests of India, subject to international treaties and domestic laws of foreign states.
2. **Expansion of Scheduled Offences**:
   - Extended NIA's jurisdiction beyond terrorism to include:
     - Human trafficking (Sections 370 and 370A IPC).
     - Offences related to Counterfeit Currency or bank notes (Section 489A-489E IPC).
     - Manufacture or sale of prohibited arms (Arms Act 1959).
     - **Cyber-Terrorism (Section 66F of the Information Technology Act, 2000)**.
     - Explosive Substances Act, 1908.
3. **Special Courts (Section 11 & 22)**:
   - Central and State Governments empowered to designate Sessions Courts as Special Courts for speedy trial of scheduled offences, with appeals lying directly to a Division Bench of the High Court.

## 3. Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act, 1958 (AFSPA)

### Core Statutory Mechanics
- Enacted to confer special powers upon the armed forces in "disturbed areas" in the North-Eastern States (Assam, Manipur, Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh) and later extended to Jammu & Kashmir (via AFSPA 1990).
- **Section 3 - Declaration of "Disturbed Area"**:
  - The Governor of the State, the Administrator of the Union Territory, or the **Central Government** may, by notification in the Official Gazette, declare the whole or any part of the State/UT as a disturbed area if in its opinion it is in such a dangerous condition that use of armed forces in aid of civil power is necessary.
- **Section 4 - Exceptional Operational Powers**:
  - Any commissioned officer, warrant officer, or non-commissioned officer may:
    - Fire upon or use force, **even to the causing of death**, against any person acting in contravention of any law or order prohibiting assemblies of five or more persons, carrying weapons, or fire-arms.
    - Destroy any arms dump, fortified position, or shelter from which armed attacks are made.
    - Arrest without warrant any person who has committed, or is suspected of having committed, a cognizable offence.
    - Enter and search without warrant any premises to make arrests or recover concealed arms.
- **Section 6 - Absolute Legal Immunity (The Core Controversy)**:
  - *No prosecution, suit or other legal proceeding shall be instituted, except with the previous sanction of the Central Government*, against any person in respect of anything done or purported to be done in exercise of powers conferred by this Act.

### Committees & Landmark Constitutional Jurisprudence
- **Justice B.P. Jeevan Reddy Committee (2005)**:
  - Formed by the Central Government to review AFSPA. Unanimously recommended its **complete repeal**, observing that the Act had become *"a symbol of hate, oppression, and an instrument of high-handedness"*. Recommended inserting essential provisions into the UAPA instead.
- **Justice Santosh Hegde Committee (2013)**:
  - Investigated 6 representative encounter deaths in Manipur; found that all six encounters were staged and fake, and the victims had no criminal antecedents.
- **Extra Judicial Execution Victim Families Association (EEVFAM) v. Union of India (2016 - Supreme Court)**:
  - Landmark Constitution ruling: Declared that **armed forces personnel do not enjoy blanket sovereign immunity** for offences committed under the guise of AFSPA.
  - Held that an indefinite deployment of armed forces in aid of civil power subverts democratic functioning; excess force causing death is subject to judicial inquiry, and every encounter death in a disturbed area must be investigated through an independent FIR.

## 4. Left-Wing Extremism (LWE) & The SAMADHAN Framework

### Socio-Economic Roots of the "Red Corridor"
- Spread across the Dandakaranya forest belt (Bastar, Sukma, Dantewada in Chhattisgarh; Malkangiri in Odisha; Gadchiroli in Maharashtra; Jharkhand).
- **Underlying Grievances (The Jal, Jangal, Zameen Matrix)**:
  - Historical dispossession of tribal lands by non-tribal settlers and corporate mining projects.
  - Poor implementation of the Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA) and Forest Rights Act, 2006 (FRA).
  - State absence: Vacuum in primary healthcare, schools, and fair price shops exploited by CPI (Maoist) cadres establishing parallel courts (*Jan Adalat*).

### The Government's Comprehensive Strategy: The SAMADHAN Doctrine
Formulated by the Ministry of Home Affairs in 2017:
- **S** — Smart Leadership (strategic vision and decentralized field commander initiative).
- **A** — Aggressive Strategy (active operational domination, offensive search operations).
- **M** — Motivation and Training (specialized training in jungle warfare e.g., Greyhounds of Andhra/Telangana, CoBRA battalion of CRPF, District Reserve Guard - DRG in Chhattisgarh).
- **A** — Actionable Intelligence (real-time technical intelligence, UAV reconnaissance, satellite data).
- **D** — Dashboard-based KPIs (monitoring development markers in 70 LWE-affected districts).
- **H** — Harnessing Technology (thermal imaging, GPS tracking of patrols, anti-landmine vehicles).
- **A** — Action plan for each theatre (customized geographic strategies).
- **N** — No access to financing (stopping extortion levies on tendu leaf contractors and mining firms through Enforcement Directorate PMLA crackdowns).
- **Developmental Counter-Measures**:
  - *Eklavya Model Residential Schools (EMRS)* providing quality tribal education.
  - *Road Requirement Plan (RRP-II)* constructing all-weather fortified roads connecting remote police stations and villages.
  - *Mobile Connectivity Project* installing 4G telecom towers in shadow areas.

## 5. Border Management & Coastal Security Architectures

### Comprehensive Integrated Border Management System (CIBMS)
- Modern technological transition from static physical fencing to a **"Smart Border"** integrated matrix along the western (Pakistan) and eastern (Bangladesh) borders.
- **Three Core Pillars**:
  1. *Sensory Layer*: High-resolution thermal imagers, infrared intrusion detectors, unattended ground sensors (UGS), underground vibration sensors, and underground fiber-optic acoustic monitors detecting tunneling.
  2. *Communication Layer*: Dedicated secure optical fiber cables, wireless transmission towers, and aerostat balloons providing panoramic aerial surveillance.
  3. *Command & Control Layer*: Centralized control room integrating sensor feeds with automated pan-tilt-zoom cameras and laser tripwires, alerting Quick Reaction Teams (QRTs).

### Vibrant Villages Programme (VVP, 2023)
- Centrally sponsored scheme (₹4,800 crore outlay) focusing on comprehensive development of border villages along the northern border (Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, and UT of Ladakh).
- Objectives: Stemming out-migration from sensitive high-altitude border settlements, developing round-the-year road connectivity, solar micro-grids, homestay tourism, and cooperative societies, transforming border villages into the nation's **"first line of defense"**.
