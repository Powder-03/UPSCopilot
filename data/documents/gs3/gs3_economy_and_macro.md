# GS-3: Indian Economy, Fiscal Policy & Macroeconomic Frameworks (Canonical Dossier)

## 1. Fiscal Policy & The FRBM Architecture

### The Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act, 2003 (FRBM Act)
- **Legislative Objective**: Institutionalize financial discipline, reduce fiscal deficits, enhance macroeconomic stability, and eliminate revenue deficits through multi-year fiscal budgeting targets.
- **N.K. Singh Committee Report (FRBM Review Committee, 2017)**:
  - Recommended shifting the primary fiscal anchor from the annual fiscal deficit to the **Public Debt-to-GDP Ratio**:
    - **General Government Debt Target**: **60% of GDP** (composed of **40% for the Central Government** and **20% for State Governments combined**).
  - Recommended a **Fiscal Deficit target of 3.0% of GDP** for the Centre.
  - Recommended abolishing the "Effective Revenue Deficit" concept as an accounting subterfuge.
- **The Escape Clause (Section 4(2) of amended FRBM Act)**:
  - Permits the Central Government to deviate from the fiscal deficit path by up to **0.5 percentage points** of GDP in a financial year under strictly defined exceptional grounds:
    1. Overriding considerations of national security, acts of war.
    2. National calamity of severe nature, collapse of agriculture severely affecting output.
    3. Structural reforms in the economy with unanticipated fiscal implications (e.g., GST rollout).
    4. Sharp decline in real GDP growth of at least 3 percentage points below the average of the previous 4 quarters.

## 2. Monetary Policy Framework & Inflation Targeting

### The Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT) Regime
- Formally adopted in 2016 through amendments to the **Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934** (based on the **Dr. Urjit Patel Committee Report**).
- **Statutory Mandate (Section 45ZA)**:
  - The Central Government, in consultation with the RBI, determines the inflation target in terms of the **Consumer Price Index (CPI - Combined)** once every five years.
  - **Current Inflation Target**: **4.0% with a tolerance band of +/- 2.0%** (i.e., lower bound **2.0%** and upper bound **6.0%**).
- **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC - Section 45ZB)**:
  - 6-member statutory committee responsible for setting the benchmark policy repo rate:
    - 3 Members from the RBI: RBI Governor (Chairperson, ex-officio), Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, and one officer nominated by the Central Board.
    - 3 External Members: Appointed by the Central Government on the recommendation of a Cabinet Secretary-led search committee (economists/experts of repute; 4-year tenure, not eligible for re-appointment).
  - **Decision-Making**: Decisions taken by majority vote; each member has one vote, with the RBI Governor holding a **casting vote** in the event of a tie.
- **Accountability Clause (Section 45ZN)**:
  - If average inflation breaches the tolerance band (remains >6% or <2%) for **three consecutive quarters**, the RBI must submit a formal written report to the Central Government explaining:
    1. The reasons for failure to achieve the target.
    2. Remedial actions proposed to be taken.
    3. An estimated time period within which the target will be regained.

## 3. Goods and Services Tax (GST) & Fiscal Federalism

### Constitutional Foundations (101st Amendment Act, 2016)
- **Article 246A**: Confers simultaneous power on both Parliament and State Legislatures to make laws with respect to goods and services tax. Ended the historical separation of tax powers (where the Centre taxed manufacturing/services and States taxed sale of goods).
- **Article 269A**: Levies **Integrated GST (IGST)** on interstate trade and imports, collected by the Union and apportioned between the Union and the States as per the recommendations of the GST Council.
- **Article 279A - The GST Council**:
  - A federal constitutional body tasked with all decisions regarding tax rates, exemptions, threshold limits, and dispute mechanisms.
  - **Composition**: Union Finance Minister (Chairperson), Union Minister of State for Finance, and State Finance/Taxation Ministers.
  - **Voting Weightage**:
    - Central Government: **One-third (33.3%)** of the total votes cast.
    - State Governments combined: **Two-thirds (66.7%)** of the total votes cast.
    - **Decision Threshold**: Every decision requires a **three-fourths (75%) majority** of the weighted votes present and voting. Neither the Centre alone nor the States alone can push through a decision without consensus.
- **Mohit Minerals Case (Union of India v. Mohit Minerals, 2022 - Supreme Court)**:
  - Ruled that recommendations of the GST Council are **not legally binding on Parliament and State Legislatures**, but possess persuasive value.
  - Emphasized that Indian federalism is a dialogue between cooperative federalism and uncooperative federalism, and states possess autonomous legislative authority.

## 4. Capital Expenditure (Capex) & The Investment Multiplier

### The Capex-Led Growth Strategy
- **Capex Multiplier vs Revenue Multiplier**:
  - Studies by the Reserve Bank of India and NIPFP demonstrate that **Capital Expenditure has a multiplier of ~2.45 to 3.0**, meaning every ₹1 spent on capex generates ₹2.45 to ₹3.00 of additional economic output over 2–3 years.
  - Conversely, Revenue Expenditure has a multiplier of only **~0.92 to 1.0**, delivering short-lived consumption without expanding productive capacity.
- **"Crowding-In" vs "Crowding-Out"**:
  - High public capex in trunk infrastructure (highways, dedicated freight corridors, multi-modal logistics parks, digital grids) lowers commercial logistics costs (from 14% to sub-10% of GDP) and "crowds-in" private corporate investment.
- **Scheme for Special Assistance to States for Capital Investment**:
  - The Central Government provides **50-year interest-free loans** to state governments specifically earmarked for capital expenditure projects (urban planning, police housing, industrial corridors).

## 5. Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) & India Stack

### The Three-Layered Architecture
1. **Identity Layer (Aadhaar)**: Biometric, verifiable digital identity for over 1.35 billion citizens. Serves as the foundation for the JAM Trinity (Jan Dhan, Aadhaar, Mobile) enabling Direct Benefit Transfers (DBT) that eliminated ghost beneficiaries and saved over ₹2.7 lakh crore in subsidy leakages.
2. **Payments Layer (Unified Payments Interface - UPI)**:
   - Built by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI); real-time, interoperable, mobile-based payment network handling over **13 billion transactions monthly**.
3. **Data Empowerment Layer (Account Aggregator & DigiLocker)**:
   - Consent-based financial data-sharing architecture enabling citizens and MSMEs to access collateral-free institutional credit based on cash-flow history rather than physical land assets.
4. **Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC)**:
   - An open-source protocol aimed at breaking e-commerce duopolies by unbundling search, ordering, logistics, and payment across retail.
