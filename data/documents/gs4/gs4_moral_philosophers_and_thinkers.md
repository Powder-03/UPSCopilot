# GS-4: Moral Thinkers, Ethical Philosophies & Foundational Values (Canonical Dossier)

## 1. Western Ethical Traditions & Philosophies

### Deontology: Immanuel Kant (Duty-Based Ethics)
- **Core Premise**:
  - Actions are intrinsically right or wrong, regardless of their consequences. Morality is grounded in pure reason and duty (*Deon* = Duty).
  - Rejects consequentialism: Doing the right thing for fear of punishment or hope of reward is not moral; true moral action is motivated solely by **"Duty for Duty's Sake"**.
- **The Categorical Imperative (Unconditional Moral Commands)**:
  1. **The Formula of Universal Law**:
     - *"Act only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law."*
     - If an action (e.g., lying, corruption, breaking a promise) cannot be universalized without logical contradiction or societal collapse, it is categorically immoral.
  2. **The Formula of Humanity as an End in Itself**:
     - *"Act in such a way that you treat humanity, whether in your own person or in the person of any other, never merely as a means to an end, but always at the same time as an end."*
     - Fundamental philosophical barrier against human exploitation: Prohibits treating citizens, workers, or vulnerable individuals as disposable instruments for political expediency, state profit, or public convenience.

### Consequentialism & Utilitarianism (Teleological Ethics)
- **Core Axiom**: The moral worth of an action is judged solely by its resulting consequences.
- **Jeremy Bentham: Quantitative Utilitarianism**:
  - Propounded the **Principle of Utility**: *"The greatest happiness of the greatest number is the measure of right and wrong."*
  - **Felicific / Hedonistic Calculus**: Weighed pleasures and pains purely quantitatively along 7 variables: Intensity, Duration, Certainty, Propinquity, Fecundity, Purity, and Extent.
  - Held all pleasures as qualitatively equal: *"Quantity of pleasure being equal, push-pin is as good as poetry."*
- **John Stuart Mill: Qualitative Utilitarianism & The Harm Principle**:
  - Refined Bentham by distinguishing **Higher Intellectual/Moral Pleasures** from **Lower Bodily/Sensual Pleasures**:
    - *"It is better to be a human being dissatisfied than a pig satisfied; better to be Socrates dissatisfied than a fool satisfied."*
  - **The Harm Principle** (*On Liberty*, 1859):
    - The only purpose for which power can be rightfully exercised over any member of a civilized community, against his will, is to **prevent harm to others**. One's own physical or moral good is not a sufficient warrant. Foundational bedrock for civil liberties and free speech.
  - **Rule Utilitarianism vs Act Utilitarianism**:
    - Act Utilitarianism applies utility to individual decisions (risks justifying injustice against minorities if majority benefits).
    - Rule Utilitarianism mandates following general moral rules (e.g., "Do not frame an innocent person") because adhering to the rule produces the greatest long-term aggregate happiness.

### Virtue Ethics: Aristotle (*Nicomachean Ethics*)
- **Core Premise**: Morality is not about following rigid rules or calculating outcomes; it is about cultivating virtuous character traits (**Virtues / *Arete***).
- **Teleology & *Eudaimonia***:
  - Every human action aims at some good; the ultimate supreme end of human life is **Eudaimonia** (human flourishing, living well, self-realization).
- **The Doctrine of the Mean (The Golden Mean)**:
  - Moral virtue is an intermediate state between two vices: one of **excess** and one of **deficiency**.
  - *Courage* is the Golden Mean between *Cowardice* (deficiency) and *Rashness/Foolhardiness* (excess).
  - *Temperance* is the Mean between *Insensibility* (deficiency) and *Self-Indulgence/Licentiousness* (excess).
  - *Generosity* is the Mean between *Meanness/Stinginess* (deficiency) and *Prodigality/Wastefulness* (excess).
- **Practical Wisdom (*Phronesis*)**: The intellectual virtue that enables a person to determine the appropriate golden mean in complex, real-world administrative circumstances.

### Justice as Fairness: John Rawls (*A Theory of Justice*, 1971)
- Reinvigorated social contract theory as a profound alternative to Utilitarianism (which Rawls criticized for sacrificing minority rights for aggregate majority welfare).
- **The Original Position & The Veil of Ignorance**:
  - A thought experiment where rational representatives deliberate the principles of justice for society from behind a **"Veil of Ignorance"** — where no one knows their social class, race, religion, gender, natural talents, or physical abilities.
  - Under these conditions of radical uncertainty, individuals choose a **"Maximin" Strategy** (maximizing the welfare of the minimum/worst-off position) to protect themselves against the risk of ending up at the bottom of the social pyramid.
- **The Two Principles of Justice**:
  1. **The Greatest Equal Liberty Principle**: Each person has an equal right to the most extensive basic liberties compatible with a similar liberty for all.
  2. **Social and Economic Inequalities must satisfy two conditions**:
     - *Fair Equality of Opportunity*: Offices and positions open to all under conditions of fair opportunity.
     - **The Difference Principle**: Social and economic inequalities are permissible only if they work to the **maximum benefit of the least-advantaged members of society**. (The definitive moral justification for affirmative action, progressive taxation, and targeted welfare).

## 2. Indian Ethical Traditions & Moral Philosophers

### Mahatma Gandhi: The Moral Compass of Indian Governance
- **The Inseparability of Ends and Means**:
  - Diametrically opposed to Machiavellianism (*"The end justifies the means"*).
  - For Gandhi, ends and means are convertibles: *"The means may be likened to a seed, the end to a tree; and there is just the same inviolable connection between the means and the end as there is between the seed and the tree."* Impure means will corrupt even the most noble end.
- **The Seven Social Sins (Published in *Young India*, 1925)**:
  1. *Politics without Principles* (loss of public integrity).
  2. *Wealth without Work* (speculation, rent-seeking, exploitation).
  3. *Pleasure without Conscience* (hedonism devoid of empathy).
  4. *Knowledge without Character* (dangerous intellect lacking moral grounding).
  5. *Commerce without Morality* (unethical profiteering, adulteration, cartels).
  6. *Science without Humanity* (weapons of mass destruction, bio-ethics collapse).
  7. *Worship without Sacrifice* (ritualistic religiosity without ethical conduct).
- **Gandhi's Talisman (The Civil Servant's Ethical Compass, August 1947)**:
  - *"Whenever you are in doubt, or when the self becomes too much with you, apply the following test:*
    *Recall the face of the poorest and the weakest man whom you may have seen, and ask yourself, if the step you contemplate is going to be of any use to him. Will he gain anything by it? Will it restore him to a control over his own life and destiny? In other words, will it lead to swaraj for the hungry and spiritually starving millions?*
    *Then you will find your doubts and your self melt away."*
- **Doctrine of Trusteeship (*Aparigraha*)**:
  - Wealthy individuals and capitalists should regard themselves not as exclusive owners of their riches, but as **trustees holding wealth in sacred trust for the society** that enabled its creation. Surplus wealth must be voluntarily dedicated to public welfare and community upliftment.
- **Sarvodaya through Antyodaya**:
  - Progress of all (*Sarvodaya*) can only be measured by the upliftment and empowerment of the last person standing in the socioeconomic queue (*Antyodaya*).

### Kautilya (Chanakya): *Arthashastra* & Statecraft Ethics
- **Yogakshema (The Welfare State Paradigm)**:
  - Reconciled political realism (*Dandaniti*) with the supreme welfare of the citizenry:
    - *"Prajasukhe sukham rajnah prajanam cha hite hitam,*
      *Natmapriyam hitam rajnah prajanam tu priyam hitam."*
    - **"In the happiness of his subjects lies the king's happiness; in their welfare his welfare. The king shall not consider as good only that which pleases him, but treat as beneficial to himself whatever causes happiness to his subjects."**
- **Public Accountability & Surveillance**:
  - Detailed mechanisms to audit public treasuries, detect 40 types of embezzlement by state officials, and enforce severe deterrence against public servant corruption.

### Swami Vivekananda: Practical Vedanta & Character Ethics
- **Neo-Vedanta / Practical Vedanta**:
  - Moved spirituality out of ascetic caves into the social arena of public service: *"They alone live who live for others, the rest are more dead than alive."*
  - **Jiva is Shiva**: Serving a living human being is equivalent to worshipping the divine.
  - Character is the aggregate of moral habits; emphasized the virtues of **Fearlessness (*Abhaya*)**, physical vigor, intellectual sharpness, and deep universal compassion.

### Thiruvalluvar (*Thirukkural*): Ethics in Public Administration
- Masterpiece of ancient Tamil ethical literature organized into *Aram* (Virtue/Dharma), *Porul* (Wealth/Governance), and *Inbam* (Love).
- **Virtues of an Ideal Administrator/Ruler**:
  - A ruler must possess four qualities: Access to resources, ability to acquire them, wisdom to protect them, and righteousness to distribute them fairly for public welfare.
  - Declares that sovereignty is sustained not by the army or scepter, but by the **uncompromising impartiality of justice** (*Chenkol*).
