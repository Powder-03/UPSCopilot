# GS-4: Ethics in Governance, Probity in Public Life & Anti-Corruption Frameworks (Canonical Dossier)

## 1. The Nolan Committee: Seven Principles of Public Life

Formulated in 1995 by the Committee on Standards in Public Life chaired by **Lord Nolan** (United Kingdom); globally recognized as the foundational ethical standard for civil servants and holders of public office:

1. **Selflessness**:
   - Holders of public office should act solely in terms of the **public interest**.
   - They must never act to gain financial or other material benefits for themselves, their family, or their friends.
2. **Integrity**:
   - Holders of public office must avoid placing themselves under any financial or other obligation to outside individuals or organizations that might seek to influence them in the performance of their official duties.
3. **Objectivity**:
   - In carrying out public business, including making public appointments, awarding contracts, or recommending individuals for rewards and benefits, holders of public office must make choices **strictly on merit, evidence, and impartial criteria**.
4. **Accountability**:
   - Holders of public office are accountable for their decisions and actions to the public and must submit themselves to whatever scrutiny is appropriate to their office.
5. **Openness**:
   - Holders of public office should act and take decisions in an open and transparent manner.
   - Information should not be withheld from the public unless there are clear and lawful reasons so to do.
6. **Honesty**:
   - Holders of public office have a duty to declare any private interests relating to their public duties and to take steps to resolve any conflicts arising in a way that protects the public interest.
7. **Leadership**:
   - Holders of public office should exhibit these principles in their own behavior and actively promote and robustly support the principles by leadership and example, challenging poor behavior wherever it occurs.

## 2. The 2nd Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC): 4th Report ("Ethics in Governance")

### Fundamental Recommendations
- **Code of Ethics vs Code of Conduct**:
  - *Code of Ethics*: A broad, values-driven aspirational statement articulating overarching moral principles that guide civil service behavior (e.g., Integrity, Compassion, Empathy for the marginalized, Non-partisanship, Commitment to the Constitution).
  - *Code of Conduct*: A specific, rule-based prescriptive list of concrete "dos and don'ts" backed by formal departmental disciplinary sanctions (e.g., Central Civil Services Conduct Rules 1964).
  - The 2nd ARC recommended enacting a comprehensive statutory **Public Services Bill** to give legal force to a national Code of Ethics alongside the existing Code of Conduct.
- **Reforming Article 311 of the Constitution**:
  - Article 311 provides procedural safeguards against dismissal, removal, or reduction in rank of civil servants.
  - The Commission noted that while protection against arbitrary political victimization is essential, Article 311 has historically been abused as a legal shield by corrupt officers to stall departmental proceedings for decades.
  - Recommended amending Article 311 so that in cases where a public servant is caught red-handed in a trap or where *prima facie* evidence of corruption exists, they can be summarily removed following a swift, time-bound preliminary inquiry.
- **Confiscation of Illegally Acquired Assets**:
  - Mere imprisonment is insufficient deterrence; recommended enacting an overarching statute providing for the **immediate attachment and confiscation of properties and assets** of public servants that are disproportionate to their known sources of income, reversing the burden of proof onto the corrupt official.
- **Whistleblower Protection**:
  - Recommended robust statutory protection for whistleblowers, insulating honest public servants who expose institutional corruption from physical violence, administrative transfers, and career harassment.

## 3. Central Civil Services (Conduct) Rules, 1964

### Core Behavioral Mandates
- **Rule 3 - General Cardinal Principles**:
  - *Rule 3(1)(i)*: Maintain absolute integrity at all times.
  - *Rule 3(1)(ii)*: Maintain devotion to duty.
  - *Rule 3(1)(iii)*: Do nothing which is unbecoming of a government servant.
  - *Rule 3(1)(iv)*: Commit himself to and uphold the supremacy of the Constitution and democratic values.
  - *Rule 3(1)(v)*: Defend and uphold the sovereignty and integrity of India.
  - *Rule 3(1)(vi)*: Maintain political neutrality.
  - *Rule 3(1)(vii)*: Promote principles of merit, fairness, and impartiality in discharge of duties.
  - *Rule 3(1)(viii)*: Maintain high ethical standards, transparency, and accountability.
  - *Rule 3(1)(ix)*: Maintain responsiveness to the public, particularly towards the weaker sections.
- **Conflict of Interest Restrictions**:
  - **Rule 4**: Government servant shall not use their position or influence directly or indirectly to secure employment for any member of their family in any private company or firm having official dealings with them.
  - **Rule 13 - Gifts**: Strict monetary caps on acceptance of gifts from personal friends/relatives on occasions like weddings without official government sanction.
  - **Rule 15 - Private Trade & Employment**: No government servant shall, without previous sanction of the Government, engage directly or indirectly in any trade or business, or negotiate for, or undertake, any other employment.
  - **Rule 16 - Investments & Lending**: Strict prohibition on speculative stock trading and lending or borrowing money to/from persons within official jurisdiction.
  - **Rule 3C - Prohibition of Sexual Harassment of Working Women**: Mandatory compliance with POSH Act guidelines within government offices.

## 4. Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (POCA) & The 2018 Paradigm Shift

### Legislative Genesis & Landmark 2018 Amendments
- Enacted to consolidate and amend the law relating to the prevention of corruption and to make procedural deterrence effective.
- **The Historic 2018 Amendment Act**:
  - Radically shifted anti-corruption jurisprudence by penalizing both the supply side and demand side of corruption:
  1. **Section 7 - Bribe Taking by Public Servant**:
     - Criminalizes the act of demanding, accepting, or attempting to obtain any undue advantage with the intention to perform or cause performance of a public duty improperly or dishonestly. Punishable with imprisonment of **3 to 7 years**.
  2. **Section 7A - Influence Peddling**:
     - Penalizes any intermediary or third-party middleman who accepts undue advantage to induce a public servant by corrupt or illegal means.
  3. **Section 8 - Bribe Giving Made an Explicit Offence**:
     - Under the original 1988 Act, the bribe-giver was treated merely as a victim or abettor. The 2018 amendment made bribe-giving a **direct, substantive criminal offence**.
     - **Safe-Harbor Clause (7-Day Reporting Window)**: If a person is coerced into giving a bribe, they will not be prosecuted provided they report the matter to a law enforcement agency within **seven days** of giving the bribe.
  4. **Section 9 - Corporate Bribery Liability**:
     - Commercial organizations (corporations, LLPs, partnerships) are held criminally liable if any person associated with the organization bribes a public servant to obtain or retain business, unless the organization proves that it had in place **adequate compliance and anti-bribery procedures**.
  5. **Section 17A - Mandatory Prior Sanction for Investigation (The Protection Clause)**:
     - No police officer shall conduct any enquiry, inquiry, or investigation into any offence alleged to have been committed by a public servant where the alleged offence is relatable to **any recommendation made or decision taken by such public servant in the discharge of official functions or duties**, without the **previous approval of the competent authority**.
     - *Exception*: No prior approval is required in cases involving the arrest of a public servant on the spot on the charge of accepting or attempting to accept any undue advantage for himself or another person (trap cases).
     - *Legislative Intent*: Protects honest civil servants from frivolous complaints and policy paralysis, preventing vindictive investigations into bonafide administrative decisions.
