# NCERT Class 11: An Introduction to Indian Art, Part 1 (Core Conceptual Extract)

## 1. Prehistoric Rock Paintings & Indus Valley Civilization

### Prehistoric Rock Paintings (Bhimbetka Caves, MP)
- Discovered by V.S. Wakankar (1957) in the Vindhyan ranges of Madhya Pradesh.
- **Upper Paleolithic**: Linear representations of huge animal figures (bisons, elephants, tigers, rhinos) with geometric wash designs in green and dark red pigments.
- **Mesolithic Period (Largest Concentration)**:
  - Smaller size paintings depicting communal hunting scenes, hand-held spears, bows and arrows.
  - Social life motifs: Family scenes, women grinding corn, communal dancing, children playing.
  - Colors derived from natural minerals: Red from Geru (haematite/iron oxide), White from limestone/chalk, Green from chalcedony.

### Indus Valley Civilization (Bronze Age Art & Urbanism)
- **Town Planning**: Grid pattern with streets intersecting at right angles; advanced subterranean covered drainage system. Division into a raised western **Citadel** (public buildings, administrative complexes) and an eastern **Lower Town** (residential quarters).
  - **Great Bath (Mohenjo-Daro)**: Watertight brickwork using bitumen mortar with changing rooms on three sides.
  - **Lothal (Gujarat)**: Tidal dockyard connected to the Sabarmati river basin, warehouse structures, and Persian Gulf seals.
  - **Dholavira (Kutch)**: Unique tripartite division (Citadel, Middle Town, Lower Town), monumental stone architecture, elaborate water harvesting reservoirs, and large wooden signboard with Indus script.
- **Sculpture & Metallurgical Arts**:
  - **Bronze Dancing Girl (Mohenjo-Daro)**: Cast using the **Lost-Wax Technique (*Cire Perdue*)**; tribhanga posture, right hand on hip, left arm adorned with bangles, naturalistic and expressive anatomy.
  - **Steatite Priest-King (Mohenjo-Daro)**: Bearded elite draped in an embroidered shawl with a trefoil pattern, elongated half-closed meditative eyes.
  - **Terracotta Art**: Mother Goddess figurines with elaborate fan-shaped headdresses; toy carts with movable wheels.
  - **Seals**: Carved mostly from steatite, chert, and faience. **Pashupati Seal (Mohenjo-Daro)**: Seated cross-legged figure in yogic posture wearing a horned headdress, surrounded by four animals (Elephant, Tiger, Rhinoceros, Buffalo) and two deer beneath the seat.

## 2. Mauryan & Post-Mauryan Architecture

### Mauryan Court Art vs Folk Art
- **Court Art (State-Sponsored by Ashoka)**:
  - **Monolithic Ashokan Pillars**: Carved from single blocks of Chunar sandstone, characterized by a lustrous glassy polish (*Mauryan Polish*).
  - **Anatomy of the Sarnath Lion Capital** (National Emblem of India):
    1. Inverted lotus / bell-shaped base.
    2. Circular abacus carved with four animals representing cardinal directions: Bull (West), Horse (South), Elephant (East), Lion (North).
    3. Intermediate wheels with 24 spokes (**Dharmachakra**).
    4. Four majestic Asiatic lions seated back-to-back, symbolizing universal sovereignty (*Chakravartin*).
  - **Rock-Cut Caves**: Earliest rock-cut architecture in India in the Barabar and Nagarjuni hills (Bihar), patronized by Ashoka and Dasharatha for the **Ajivika sect** (e.g., Lomas Rishi Cave with its chaitya arch facade, Sudama Cave).
- **Folk / Individual Art**:
  - Monumental stone Yaksha and Yakshini sculptures associated with fertility, wealth, and nature worship.
  - **Didarganj Yakshini** (Patna): Polished Chunar sandstone, holding a flywhisk (*chauri*), full-figured female anatomy reflecting classic Indian aesthetic canonical proportions.

### Stupa Architecture & Anatomy
- **Origin**: Evolved from pre-Buddhist burial mounds into Buddhist commemorative and relic shrines.
- **Canonical Components of a Stupa**:
  1. **Medhi**: Circular or square elevated drum/plinth supporting the stupa.
  2. **Anda**: Semi-circular hemispherical dome representing the celestial dome and universe.
  3. **Harmika**: Square balcony-like railing perched atop the Anda; sacred enclosure housing the corporal relics (*Dhatu*) of the Buddha or Arhats.
  4. **Yasti**: Central axial mast rising from the Harmika.
  5. **Chhatri (Triple Umbrella)**: Mounted on the Yasti; symbolizes the Three Jewels of Buddhism (**Triratna**: Buddha, Dharma, Sangha).
  6. **Pradakshina-patha**: Circumambulatory pathway around the base, enclosed by stone railings (**Vedika**).
  7. **Torana**: Monumental sculpted ceremonial gateways erected at the four cardinal directions (e.g., Sanchi Stupa No. 1, Bharhut, Amaravati). Sculpted with Jataka tales, life episodes of Buddha, and Salabhanjika bracket figures.

## 3. Schools of Sculpture & Cave Traditions

### Comparison of the Three Major Sculpture Schools

| Feature | Gandhara School | Mathura School | Amaravati School |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Geographical Area** | North-West Frontier (Peshawar, Taxila, Swat) | Mathura (Uttar Pradesh) | Krishna-Godavari Valley (Andhra Pradesh) |
| **Patrons** | Kushana Rulers | Kushana Rulers | Satavahana and Ikshvaku Rulers |
| **Material Used** | Blue-grey schist rock; Stucco in later phases | Spotted red sandstone | White marble / limestone |
| **Religious Influence**| Strictly Buddhist (Mahayana) | Multi-religious: Buddhist, Jain (Ayagapatas), and Hindu (Vishnu, Shiva, Surya) | Exclusively Buddhist (Hinayana & Mahayana) |
| **Stylistic Features** | Hellenistic / Greco-Roman influence: Apollo-like facial features, wavy curly hair, sharp nose, muscular physique, heavy drapery with Roman folds. | Indigenous Indian aesthetic: Chubby face, smiling expression, shaved head with topknot, transparent thin drapery clinging to body, masculine physical power. | Dynamic narrative relief art: Less emphasis on individual statues, intense group compositions depicting Jataka scenes with fluid human movement. |

### Rock-Cut Caves: Chaityas vs Viharas
- **Chaitya**: Buddhist prayer and congregational assembly hall featuring an apsidal plan, a barrel-vaulted ceiling, a stupa at the rear focus, and colonnaded side aisles for pradakshina (e.g., Karle Chaitya — largest in India with Lion pillar; Bhaja, Kanheri).
- **Vihara**: Monastic residential quarters with a central open courtyard surrounded by small living cells for monks (e.g., Ajanta Caves, Ellora, Nasik).
- **Ajanta Caves (Maharashtra)**: 29 caves carved into a horse-shoe shaped gorge along the Waghora river. Paintings executed using the **Fresco-secco technique** on lime-plastered rock walls. Famous murals: Bodhisattva Padmapani (holding lotus, embodying compassion) and Bodhisattva Vajrapani (holding thunderbolt); Dying Princess.
- **Ellora Caves (Maharashtra)**: 34 caves representing religious harmony: Buddhist (Caves 1–12), Hindu (Caves 13–29), and Jain (Caves 30–34).
  - **Kailash Temple (Cave 16)**: Monolithic rock-cut architectural marvel carved top-to-bottom from a single basalt hill under Rashtrakuta King Krishna I; replicates Mount Kailash with multi-story Dravida vimana and Ravana shaking Mount Kailash relief.

## 4. Indian Temple Architecture Styles

### Fundamental Elements of a Hindu Temple
1. **Garbhagriha (Sanctum Sanctorum)**: Small, dark, cubic chamber housing the principal deity icon (*Mula Murti*).
2. **Mandapa**: Pillared assembly hall leading to the Garbhagriha for devotees.
3. **Antarala (Vestibule)**: Intermediate transition lobby connecting the Mandapa and Garbhagriha.
4. **Shikhara / Vimana**: Towering superstructure directly above the Garbhagriha.
5. **Amalaka**: Ribbed stone disc crowning the shikhara in North Indian temples.
6. **Kalasha**: Pitcher-shaped finial placed atop the Amalaka.
7. **Vahana**: Mount of the presiding deity placed along the central visual axis in the entrance pavilion.

### Nagara Style (North Indian Architecture)
- Built on raised stone platforms (**Jagati**).
- **Absence of boundary walls and entrance gateways (Gopurams)**.
- Multiple superstructures: Mandapas have progressively rising lower roofs leading to the highest Shikhara over the sanctum.
- **Sub-Types of Shikhara**:
  - **Latina / Rekha-Prasada**: Square base with curved convex walls rising to a top point (simplest and commonest).
  - **Phamsana**: Broader base, lower height, roof constructed of straight sloping slabs rising stepped upwards.
  - **Valabhi**: Rectangular base with a barrel-vaulted roof (wagon-vaulted).
  - **Shekari**: Multi-spired composition where miniature shikharas cluster around the central tower (e.g., Khajuraho).
- **Regional Sub-Schools**:
  - **Odisha School**: Tower called **Deul** (Rekha Deul for sanctum, Pidha Deul for assembly hall / Jagamohana); plain interior with lavishly sculpted exterior walls (e.g., Lingaraja Temple Bhubaneswar, Sun Temple Konark with stone chariot wheels).
  - **Khajuraho / Chandela School**: Temples built on continuous high plinths; **Panchayatana Layout** (one central sanctum surrounded by four subsidiary shrines); interlocking stone joinery without mortar; sensual Mithuna sculptures symbolizing human desire within cosmic harmony (e.g., Kandariya Mahadeva Temple).
  - **Solanki School (Gujarat/Rajasthan)**: Elaborate stepwells (**Vavs**, e.g., Rani ki Vav); Sun Temple Modhera with stepped sacred water tank (**Surya Kund**) lined with 108 miniature shrines; intricate translucent marble carvings at Dilwara Jain Temples, Mount Abu.

### Dravida Style (South Indian Architecture)
- Enclosed within high boundary walls with monumental entrance gateways (**Gopurams**).
- **Vimana**: Stepped, pyramidal tower rising geometrically in diminishing tiers (**Talas**), capped by an octagonal or domical crowning stone (**Shikhara / Stupika**). Unlike Nagara temples, the main Vimana is single, while entrance Gopurams often exceed the Vimana in height in later periods.
- **Temple Tank (Kalyani / Pushkarini)**: Mandatory water reservoir within the temple compound for ritual ablutions.
- Presence of fierce guardian deities (**Dwarapalas**) flanking the entrance.
- **Evolutionary Dynasty Phases**:
  - **Pallava Dynasty**: Monolithic rock-cut **Pancha Rathas** and Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram; Kailasanatha Temple Kanchipuram (introduction of structural stone temples).
  - **Chola Dynasty (Golden Zenith)**: Monumental structural grandeur. **Brihadisvara Temple at Thanjavur** built by Rajaraja Chola I (1010 CE): 66-meter high Vimana, massive monolithic granite cupola weighing 80 tonnes, gigantic monolithic Nandi pavilion; Gangaikonda Cholapuram by Rajendra I.
  - **Vijayanagara Dynasty**: Introduction of **Kalyana Mandapa** (elaborate marriage pavilions with carved monolithic rearing horse pillars) and **Amman Shrines**; soaring Raya Gopurams.
  - **Nayaka Style**: Famous for gigantic corridors with elaborately sculpted pillars (e.g., Meenakshi Sundareswarar Temple Madurai, Ramanathaswamy Temple Rameswaram with 1.2 km pillared corridor).

### Vesara Style (Hybrid / Deccan Architecture)
- Promoted by the **Chalukyas of Badami**, **Rashtrakutas**, and **Hoysalas**.
- Combines Nagara spires and Dravida stepped tiers, frequently adopting an elliptical or stellate (star-shaped) ground plan.
- **Hoysala Temples (Belur, Halebidu, Somnathpur)**:
  - Built on star-shaped raised platforms (**Jagati**) using soft chloritic schist (soapstone), enabling microscopic, jewelry-like relief carvings.
  - Horizontal friezes carved with charging elephants, horses, mythical beasts (*Yalis*), swans, and celestial maidens (*Madanikas*).
