## 1. Geomorphology & Earth's Interior

### Structure of the Earth's Interior
- **Crust**: 
  - Oceanic crust: ~5 km average thickness, composed primarily of basalt (Simatic: Silica and Magnesium), denser (~3.0 g/cm³).
  - Continental crust: ~30-70 km thickness (thickest under Himalayan mountain ranges), composed of granitic rock (Sialic: Silica and Aluminium), mean density ~2.7 g/cm³.
- **Mantle**:
  - Extends from Moho discontinuity to a depth of 2,900 km.
  - **Asthenosphere**: Upper portion of the mantle extending up to 400 km; ductile, semi-molten state; primary source of magma that surfaces during volcanic eruptions.
  - **Lithosphere**: Rigid outer layer comprising the crust and the uppermost solid mantle; thickness ranges between 10 km and 200 km.
- **Core**:
  - Boundary located at 2,900 km (Gutenberg discontinuity).
  - Outer core is in a **liquid/molten state**; inner core is in a **solid state** under immense pressure.
  - Composed of heavy materials: Nickel and Iron (Nife layer), density at the center reaches ~13 g/cm³.

### Seismic Waves and Shadow Zones
- **Primary Waves (P-waves)**:
  - Longitudinal/compressional waves; particles vibrate parallel to the direction of wave propagation.
  - Travel through **all mediums: solids, liquids, and gases**; fastest seismic waves.
  - **P-wave Shadow Zone**: Due to refraction at the mantle-core boundary (2,900 km), P-waves do not arrive directly between **105° and 145°** from the earthquake epicenter.
- **Secondary Waves (S-waves)**:
  - Transverse/shear waves; particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of propagation in a vertical plane.
  - Travel **only through solid materials**; cannot penetrate liquids.
  - **S-wave Shadow Zone**: S-waves cannot pass through the liquid outer core; consequently, the shadow zone of S-waves is vast, covering the entire zone beyond **105° from the epicenter** (spanning over 40% of the Earth's surface).

### Continental Drift and Plate Tectonics
- **Continental Drift Theory (Alfred Wegener, 1912)**:
  - Posited all continents formed a single continental mass named **Pangaea**, surrounded by a mega-ocean **Panthalassa**.
  - Split into **Laurasia** (northern mass) and **Gondwanaland** (southern mass).
  - Evidence: Matching of shorelines (Jigsaw fit of South America and Africa), Tillite deposits (Gondwana glaciations), Placer gold deposits of Ghana derived from Brazilian plateau veins, Distribution of fossils (Glossopteris flora, Mesosaurus).
- **Plate Tectonics Theory (McKenzie, Parker, and Morgan, 1967)**:
  - The lithosphere is broken into major and minor tectonic plates floating over the asthenosphere driven by **convection cells** (Arthur Holmes).
  - **Major Plates (7)**: Pacific Plate, North American Plate, South American Plate, Eurasian Plate, African Plate, Antarctic Plate, Indo-Australian Plate.
  - **Plate Boundaries**:
    1. **Divergent Boundaries (Constructive)**: Plates pull apart; magma wells up from mantle to create new oceanic crust (e.g., Mid-Atlantic Ridge, East African Rift).
    2. **Convergent Boundaries (Destructive)**: One plate subducts beneath another into the asthenosphere along a subduction zone / deep ocean trench (e.g., Mariana Trench, Himalayan continent-continent collision, Andes ocean-continent collision).
    3. **Transform Boundaries (Conservative)**: Plates slide past each other horizontally along transform faults; crust is neither created nor destroyed (e.g., San Andreas Fault, California).

## 2. Climatology

### Structure of the Atmosphere
- **Troposphere**: Lowest layer (average height 13 km; 8 km at poles, 18 km at equator due to strong convectional currents). Contains >75% of atmospheric mass and all weather phenomena. Temperature decreases with altitude at the Normal Lapse Rate (~6.5°C per 1,000 meters).
- **Stratosphere**: Extends up to 50 km. Contains the **Ozone Layer** (O3) between 15 and 35 km which absorbs harmful solar ultraviolet (UV) radiation. Temperature increases with height (thermal inversion). Ideal for jet aircraft navigation due to absence of turbulence.
- **Mesosphere**: Extends from 50 km to 80 km. Temperature decreases with height down to -100°C (coldest layer). Meteors burn up in this layer upon atmospheric entry.
- **Thermosphere / Ionosphere**: Extends between 80 km and 400 km. Contains electrically charged ions; reflects radio waves back to Earth, enabling wireless communication. Auroras (Aurora Borealis and Australis) occur here.
- **Exosphere**: Outermost layer; extremely low atmospheric density gradually merging with outer space.

### Atmospheric Circulation and Wind Systems
- **Tricellular Meridional Circulation**:
  1. **Hadley Cell**: Thermally driven cell in the tropics. Air rises at the Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ), flows poleward in the upper troposphere, sinks at the Subtropical High Pressure Belts (~30° N/S), and returns toward the equator as **Trade Winds** (Northeasterly in Northern Hemisphere, Southeasterly in Southern Hemisphere).
  2. **Ferrel Cell**: Mid-latitude cell between 30° and 60° latitude. Driven mechanically by adjacent cells. Surface winds flow poleward and deflect eastward to form the **Westerlies**.
  3. **Polar Cell**: Thermally driven high-latitude cell. Cold air sinks at the Polar Highs, moves equatorward as **Polar Easterlies**, and rises at the Subpolar Low Pressure Belt (~60° N/S).
- **Jet Streams**: Narrow bands of high-altitude (9–12 km), high-velocity (>120 km/h) westerly winds in the upper troposphere.
  - Subtropical Westerly Jet Stream (STWJ): Anchors over North India during winter, splits around the Tibetan plateau, and withdraws abruptly northward during the onset of the Southwest Indian Monsoon.
  - Tropical Easterly Jet Stream (TEJ): Steered by heating of the Tibetan plateau during summer; associated with active monsoon bursts over the Indian subcontinent.

### Tropical Cyclones vs Extra-Tropical Cyclones
- **Tropical Cyclones**:
  - Thermal origin; fueled exclusively by latent heat of condensation released over warm sea surfaces (Sea Surface Temperature $\ge 27^\circ\text{C}$).
  - Characterized by a calm, cloudless central **"Eye"** surrounded by an intense spiraling **Eye Wall** of cumulonimbus clouds with violent gale-force winds.
  - Confined strictly to tropical oceans (5° to 30° N/S); do not form at the equator due to zero Coriolis force.
  - Move from East to West driven by tropical easterly trade winds.
- **Extra-Tropical / Frontal Cyclones**:
  - Dynamic origin; formed along the Polar Front where warm tropical air masses meet cold polar air masses (Frontogenesis).
  - Can form over both land and sea in mid-to-high latitudes (35° to 65°).
  - Characterized by distinct warm and cold fronts; lack a calm eye. Move from West to East guided by the Westerlies.

### Köppen Climate Classification (Indian Subcontinent Context)
- **Amw (Tropical Monsoon with short dry winter)**: Western coastal plains of India, south of Goa. Heavy monsoon rainfall (>250 cm).
- **As (Tropical Wet with dry summer)**: Coromandel Coast (Tamil Nadu and adjoining Andhra Pradesh). Receives dominant rainfall during winter from the retreating Northeast Monsoon.
- **Aw (Tropical Savanna with dry winter)**: Peninsular plateau region south of the Tropic of Cancer, excluding the Coromandel coast and semi-arid rain-shadow belt.
- **BShw (Semi-Arid Steppe)**: Rain-shadow belt east of the Western Ghats (parts of Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana) and parts of Rajasthan/Gujarat.
- **BWhw (Hot Desert)**: Extreme western Rajasthan (Thar Desert). Rainfall < 30 cm.
- **Cwg (Monsoon with dry winter and hot summer)**: Northern Gangetic plains, Assam, and parts of Rajasthan.
- **Dfc (Cold humid winter with short summer)**: Arunachal Pradesh and adjoining North-Eastern mountain regions.
- **E / ET (Polar / Tundra)**: Higher reaches of Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand.

## 3. Oceanography

### Ocean Floor Relief
1. **Continental Shelf**: Gently sloping seaward extension of continental mass (average gradient $1^\circ$ or less). Shallowest part of ocean (depth 120–200 m). Richest zone for marine living resources (phytoplankton blooming due to sunlight penetration) and offshore hydrocarbon reserves (e.g., Mumbai High).
2. **Continental Slope**: Connects the continental shelf and ocean basins; gradient steepens to $2^\circ - 5^\circ$. Submarine canyons and trenches incise this zone.
3. **Continental Rise**: Gentle depositional incline formed by sediment turbidite flows from the continental slope.
4. **Abyssal Plains / Deep Sea Plains**: Flat, extensive plains between 3,000 and 6,000 m depth. Covered with fine-grained pelagic clays and biogenous oozes; host poly-metallic nodules (manganese, nickel, copper, cobalt).
5. **Mid-Oceanic Ridges**: Interconnected submarine mountain chains stretching over 65,000 km across the globe. Center of seafloor spreading characterized by central rift valleys and basaltic volcanism.

### Ocean Currents & Thermohaline Circulation
- **Driving Forces**: Primary forces (solar heating causing water expansion, Coriolis deflection, planetary winds, gravity); Secondary forces (salinity and temperature density differentials).
- **Major Ocean Currents**:
  - **Atlantic**: Gulf Stream (Warm), North Atlantic Drift (Warm, keeps Western European ports ice-free), Canaries Current (Cold, contributes to Sahara aridity), Labrador Current (Cold, meets Gulf Stream at Grand Banks generating fog and rich fishing grounds).
  - **Pacific**: Kuroshio (Warm), Oyashio (Cold, productive fishing zone at convergence off Japan), California Current (Cold), Peru/Humboldt Current (Cold, anchors Atacama desert).
  - **Indian Ocean Seasonal Reversal**: Driven entirely by monsoon wind reversal. In summer: Southwest Monsoon Current (flows West to East); in winter: Northeast Monsoon Drift (flows East to West).
- **El Niño Southern Oscillation (ENSO)**:
  - **Normal Conditions**: Strong trade winds push warm surface water westward into the Western Pacific Warm Pool (near Indonesia); deep cold water upwells along Peru coast, supporting anchovy fisheries.
  - **El Niño (Warm Phase)**: Trade winds weaken; warm water sloshes eastward towards the South American coast. Upwelling ceases off Peru. Atmospheric convection shifts eastward, suppressing Indian monsoon convective rainfall (linked to Indian droughts).
  - **La Niña (Cold Phase)**: Trade winds intensify beyond normal; excessive warm water piles up in the Western Pacific; enhanced Peruvian upwelling. Typically associated with bountiful Indian monsoon rains.
  - **Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD)**: Positive IOD (warm Western Indian Ocean, cold Eastern Indian Ocean near Sumatra) offsets the negative impact of El Niño on the Indian Monsoon.
