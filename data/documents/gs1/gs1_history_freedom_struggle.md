# GS-1: Modern Indian History & The Freedom Struggle (Canonical Dossier)

## 1. The 1857 Uprising: Nature, Dynamics & Aftermath
- **Multi-Dimensional Causes**:
  - *Political*: Lord Dalhousie's **Doctrine of Lapse** (annexation of Satara, Sambalpur, Jhansi, Nagpur); Annexation of Awadh (1856) on grounds of "maladministration", causing deep disaffection among Awadh sepoys ("the nursery of the Bengal Army").
  - *Administrative & Economic*: Heavy land revenue assessments (Ryotwari, Mahalwari), destruction of traditional handicrafts, peasant indebtedness, exclusion of Indians from higher administrative and military posts.
  - *Social & Religious*: General Service Enlistment Act (1856) requiring sepoys to cross the seas (violating caste purity); fear of evangelical proselytization; introduction of the **Enfield Rifle** with cartridges greased with animal fat (beef and pork).
- **Key Leadership Centers**: Delhi (Bahadur Shah Zafar, General Bakht Khan), Kanpur (Nana Saheb, Tatya Tope), Lucknow (Begum Hazrat Mahal), Jhansi (Rani Lakshmibai - "I shall not surrender my Jhansi"), Bihar/Arrah (Kunwar Singh), Faizabad (Maulvi Ahmadullah).
- **Historiographical Interpretations**:
  - *British Imperialist View* (John Lawrence, Sir John Seeley): "Wholly unpatriotic and selfish Sepoy Mutiny with no native leadership and no popular support."
  - *Nationalist View* (V.D. Savarkar): "The Indian War of Independence of 1857."
  - *Balanced Critical View* (Dr. S.N. Sen, R.C. Majumdar): Started as a fight for religion and army grievances, ended as a war for independence, but lacked modern pan-Indian national consciousness.
- **Structural Transformation (Government of India Act, 1858 & Queen's Proclamation)**:
  - Liquidation of the East India Company; direct assumption of Indian governance by the British Crown.
  - Abolition of the Board of Control and Court of Directors; creation of the office of the **Secretary of State for India** assisted by a 15-member India Council.
  - **Peel Commission (Army Reorganization)**: Policy of "divide and rule" introduced in military recruitment. European-to-Indian soldier ratio drastically increased (1:2 in Bengal); martial vs non-martial race theory introduced.
  - Abandonment of territorial annexation; Indian princely rulers recognized as vassals of the British Crown (*Policy of Subordinate Union*).

## 2. Socio-Religious Reform Movements (19th Century)

### Reformist Movements (Modern Rationalist Synthesis)
- **Brahmo Samaj (Raja Ram Mohan Roy, 1828)**:
  - Pioneered the "Father of Modern Indian Renaissance". Combined rationalist monotheism with social reform.
  - Anti-Sati campaign culminated in **Regulation XVII of 1829** (Lord William Bentinck) declaring Sati illegal and punishable as culpable homicide.
  - Journalistic pioneer: *Mirat-ul-Akhbar* (Persian) and *Sambad Kaumudi* (Bengali).
  - Later divided into Brahmo Samaj of India (Keshab Chandra Sen) and Adi Brahmo Samaj (Debendranath Tagore).
- **Prarthana Samaj (Atmaram Pandurang & M.G. Ranade, 1867, Bombay)**: Focused on inter-caste dining, widow remarriage, upliftment of women and depressed classes.
- **Aligarh Movement (Sir Syed Ahmad Khan)**: Modern western scientific education for Muslims; founded the Mohammedan Anglo-Oriental College (1875) at Aligarh; advocated rational interpretation of the Quran.

### Revivalist Movements (Cultural Self-Assertion)
- **Arya Samaj (Swami Dayananda Saraswati, 1875, Bombay)**:
  - Motto: *"Go Back to the Vedas"*; rejected post-Vedic Puranic developments (idol worship, child marriage, untouchability, priestcraft).
  - Author of *Satyarth Prakash*. Established DAV (Dayanand Anglo-Vedic) institutions.
  - **Shuddhi Movement**: Reconversion of individuals who had converted from Hinduism to Islam or Christianity.
- **Ramakrishna Mission (Swami Vivekananda, 1897)**:
  - Synthesized spiritual contemplation with active humanitarian service (**Neo-Vedanta / Practical Vedanta**).
  - Keynote address at the World Parliament of Religions (Chicago, 1893): Stressed universal religious tolerance and brotherhood; declared *"Service to man is service to God"* (*Jiva is Shiva*).

### Subaltern & Anti-Caste Movements
- **Satyashodhak Samaj (Jyotirao Phule, 1873, Maharashtra)**:
  - Championed the cause of Shudras and Ati-Shudras; attacked Brahmanical hegemony and mythological subjugation.
  - Authored *Gulamgiri* (Slavery) and *Shetkaryacha Asud* (The Cultivator's Whipcord); established India's first school for lower-caste girls at Bhide Wada, Pune (1848) with his wife **Savitribai Phule**.
- **Self-Respect Movement (*Suya Mariyathai*, Periyar E.V. Ramasamy, 1925, Tamil Nadu)**:
  - Rationalist, anti-Brahmin movement; advocated non-priestly self-respect marriages, eradication of caste distinctions, and women's liberation.
- **SNDP Movement (Sri Narayana Guru, Kerala)**:
  - Propounded the radical ethical axiom: *"One Caste, One Religion, One God for All Men"* (*Oru Jathi, Oru Matham, Oru Daivam Manushyanu*). Established Shiva temple at Aruvippuram (1888) defying Brahmanical monopoly.
- **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's Anti-Caste Crusade**:
  - Established **Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha** (1924) with the clarion call: *"Educate, Agitate, Organise"*.
  - **Mahad Satyagraha (1927)**: Fought for the civic right of untouchables to draw water from the public Chhadar tank; publicly burnt the *Manusmriti*.
  - Founded the Independent Labour Party (1936) and Scheduled Castes Federation (1942).
  - Represented depressed classes in all three Round Table Conferences; signed the **Poona Pact (1932)** with Mahatma Gandhi, agreeing to reserved seats within a joint electorate instead of separate electorates.

## 3. Phases of the National Movement (1885–1947)

### Moderate Phase (1885–1905): Constitutional Agitation
- **Key Leaders**: Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta, Surendranath Banerjee, Dinshaw Wacha.
- **Methodology (3 Ps)**: Prayers, Petitions, and Protests within the confines of constitutional legality; strong faith in British sense of justice and fair play.
- **Economic Critique of Colonialism (Foundational Contribution)**:
  - **Dadabhai Naoroji**: *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India* (1876); propounded the **Drain of Wealth Theory** detailing how Indian revenues were systematically transferred to Britain as Home Charges, interest on foreign debt, and colonial pensions without any economic return.
  - Romesh Chandra Dutt (*Economic History of India*) and M.G. Ranade exposed British de-industrialization and rural pauperization.
- **Achievement**: Passage of the Indian Councils Act of 1892 (introducing budget discussion rights and indirect election elements).

### Extremist Phase & Swadeshi Movement (1905–1918)
- **Catalyst**: **Partition of Bengal (1905)** orchestrated by Viceroy Lord Curzon on administrative grounds, but designed to fracture the political nerve-center of Indian nationalism along communal lines (Hindu-majority West Bengal vs Muslim-majority East Bengal).
- **Lal-Bal-Pal Trio & Aurobindo Ghosh**:
  - Bal Gangadhar Tilak: *"Swaraj is my birthright, and I shall have it."* Transformed Ganeshotsav and Shivaji festivals into mass nationalist mobilization platforms; founded newspapers *Kesari* (Marathi) and *Mahratta* (English).
  - Bipin Chandra Pal and Lala Lajpat Rai (*The Story of My Deportation*).
  - Aurobindo Ghosh formulated the doctrine of **Passive Resistance** in *Bande Mataram*.
- **Tools of the Swadeshi Movement**: Boycott of foreign cloth, picketing of liquor shops, establishment of national educational institutions (National Council of Education, Bengal National College headed by Aurobindo), indigenous enterprises (V.O. Chidambaram Pillai's Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company, Acharya P.C. Ray's Bengal Chemicals).
- **Surat Split (1907)**: Clashes between Moderates (led by Rash Behari Ghosh, Gokhale) and Extremists (Tilak, Lajpat Rai) over extending boycott outside Bengal and resolution scope; led to the expulsion of Extremists, weakening the Congress for nearly a decade.
- **Morley-Minto Reforms (1909)**: Introduced **Separate Electorates for Muslims**, institutionalizing communalism in India's electoral architecture ("sowing dragon's teeth" - Lord Minto).
- **Home Rule Movement (1916)**: Tilak (Maharashtra, Central Provinces) and Annie Besant (rest of India) organized Home Rule Leagues demanding self-government within the British Empire; paved the way for the **Lucknow Pact (1916)** (reconciliation of Moderates and Extremists, and Congress-Muslim League joint pact).

## 4. The Gandhian Era & Mass Upsurges

### Early Experiments in Satyagraha (1917–1918)
1. **Champaran Satyagraha (1917, Bihar - First Civil Disobedience)**:
   - Protested against European indigo planters forcing peasants to cultivate indigo on 3/20th of land under the exploitative **Tinkathia System**. Gandhi secured abolition of Tinkathia and 25% refund of illegal exactions.
2. **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918, Gujarat - First Hunger Strike)**:
   - Dispute over withdrawal of Plague Bonus; Gandhi undertook a fast-unto-death, securing a 35% wage increase for mill workers.
3. **Kheda Satyagraha (1918, Gujarat - First Non-Cooperation)**:
   - Crop failure leading to peasants seeking revenue remission under the Land Revenue Code; Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel joined Gandhi; government ordered suspension of revenue collection from defaulting peasants.

### Mass Pan-Indian Movements
- **Non-Cooperation Movement (NCM, 1920–1922)**:
  - Launched in response to the Rowlatt Act ("Black Act" authorizing detention without trial), the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919 by General Dyer), and the Khilafat grievance (dismemberment of Ottoman Caliphate).
  - Program: Surrender of titles, boycott of civil services, courts, schools, and foreign goods; promotion of Khadi, Charkha, and national education (Kashi Vidyapeeth, Jamia Millia Islamia).
  - Suspended abruptly by Gandhi following the **Chauri Chaura Incident (4 February 1922)**, where an angry crowd torched a police station killing 22 policemen, upholding his unnegotiable commitment to non-violence (*Ahimsa*).
- **Civil Disobedience Movement (CDM, 1930–1934)**:
  - Preceded by the Simon Commission (1927, all-white commission), the Nehru Report (1928), and the **Lahore Session (1929)** presided by Jawaharlal Nehru where **Purna Swaraj** (Complete Independence) was declared as the goal, and the tricolor was unfurled on the banks of the Ravi River.
  - **Dandi March (12 March – 6 April 1930)**: Gandhi marched 240 miles from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi with 78 chosen followers, breaking the salt law by picking a handful of salt from the seashore. Salt chosen as the universal symbol uniting all social classes against imperial taxation.
  - Mass defiance spread: C. Rajagopalachari (Vedaranyam Salt March, Tamil Nadu), K. Kelappan (Vaikom to Payyanur, Malabar), Sarojini Naidu and Manilal Gandhi (non-violent raid on Dharasana Salt Works facing brutal police lathi charge).
  - North-West Frontier: **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan ("Frontier Gandhi")** organized the **Khudai Khidmatgars ("Red Shirts")**; soldiers of the Garhwal Rifles refused to fire upon unarmed Pathan demonstrators.
  - Suspended through the **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (March 1931)**; Gandhi attended the Second Round Table Conference in London as sole Congress representative, which ended in failure over minority quotas.
- **Quit India Movement (*Bharat Chhodo*, August 1942)**:
  - Triggered by the failure of the **Cripps Mission** (March 1942 - offering post-dated Dominion Status after WWII) and Japanese advance to the borders of Assam.
  - All-India Congress Committee passed the Quit India Resolution at **Gowalia Tank Maidan, Bombay (8 August 1942)**; Gandhi gave the historic mantra: **"Do or Die" (*Karo ya Maro*)**.
  - Immediate arrest of top Congress leadership under *Operation Zero Hour*.
  - Unprecedented, spontaneous mass upsurge: Youth, peasants, women led underground networks (Aruna Asaf Ali hoisted tricolor, Usha Mehta operated the clandestine Congress Radio, Jayaprakash Narayan escaped Hazaribagh jail to lead Azad Dasta guerillas).
  - **Parallel Governments (*Prati Sarkar*)**:
    - Ballia (UP) under Chittu Pandey.
    - Tamluk (Midnapore, Bengal) - Jatiya Sarkar with dedicated disaster relief.
    - Satara (Maharashtra) under Nana Patil and Y.B. Chavan (longest running).

### Subhas Chandra Bose & The Indian National Army (INA)
- Resigned Congress Presidency after Tripuri Session (1939) following ideological differences with Gandhi; founded the **Forward Bloc**.
- Escaped house arrest in Calcutta (1941, "The Great Escape"), traveled through Afghanistan, Soviet Union, Germany, and arrived in Tokyo/Singapore via submarine.
- Reorganized the **Azad Hind Fauj (INA)** originally formed by Mohan Singh and Rash Behari Bose.
- Established the **Provisional Government of Free India (*Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind*)** in Singapore (21 October 1943), recognized by Axis powers.
- Formed the **Rani of Jhansi Regiment** led by Captain Lakshmi Sahgal (Asia's first all-female combat unit).
- Popularized slogans: *"Dilli Chalo"*, *"Jai Hind"*, *"Tum Mujhe Khoon Do, Main Tumhe Azadi Dunga"*.
- Military campaign along Burma-Manipur border (Siege of Imphal and Kohima, 1944).
- **Post-War Impact**: The historic **INA Trials at Red Fort (1945)** of Prem Sahgal, Gurbaksh Dhillon, and Shah Nawaz Khan sparked massive nationwide naval and civilian mutinies, shattering the British military's loyalty and accelerating independence.
