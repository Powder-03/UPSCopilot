# Right to Information Act, 2005 (Act No. 22 of 2005)

## 1. Statutory Identity & Legislative Purpose
- **Full Title**: The Right to Information Act, 2005
- **Enactment Date**: 15 June 2005; Fully effective from 12 October 2005.
- **Nodal Ministry**: Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT), Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions.
- **Preamble & Objective**: An Act to provide for setting out the practical regime of right to information for citizens to secure access to information under the control of public authorities, in order to promote transparency and accountability in the working of every public authority, the constitution of a Central Information Commission and State Information Commissions, and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.
- **Constitutional Nexus**:
  - **Article 19(1)(a)**: Freedom of speech and expression encompasses the right to know and receive information (*State of UP v. Raj Narain*, 1975; *SP Gupta v. Union of India*, 1981).
  - **Article 21**: Right to life and personal liberty, governance accountability, and citizen empowerment.

---

## 2. Key Statutory Definitions (Section 2)
- **Section 2(f) - "Information"**: Any material in any form, including records, documents, memos, emails, opinions, advices, press releases, circulars, orders, logbooks, contracts, reports, papers, samples, models, data material held in any electronic form, and information relating to any private body which can be accessed by a public authority under any other law for the time being in force.
- **Section 2(h) - "Public Authority"**: Any authority, body, or institution of self-government established or constituted:
  - By or under the Constitution (e.g., President, Governors, UPSC, EC, CAG, Supreme Court/High Courts);
  - By any other law made by Parliament or State Legislature;
  - By notification issued or order made by the appropriate Government, including:
    - Bodies owned, controlled, or substantially financed;
    - Non-Government organizations (NGOs) substantially financed directly or indirectly by funds provided by the appropriate Government (*DAV College Trust case*, 2019).
- **Section 2(i) - "Record"**: Includes any document, manuscript, file, microfilm, microfiche, facsimile copy, reproduction of image, or computer-generated material.
- **Section 2(j) - "Right to Information"**: The right to information accessible under this Act held by or under the control of any public authority, including the right to:
  - Inspection of work, documents, and records;
  - Taking notes, extracts, or certified copies of documents or records;
  - Taking certified samples of material;
  - Obtaining information in electronic formats (diskettes, floppies, tapes, video cassettes, printouts).

---

## 3. Obligations of Public Authorities (Section 4)
- **Section 4(1)(a)**: Maintain all records catalogued and indexed in a manner facilitating the right to information, ensuring computerization connected across a countrywide network.
- **Section 4(1)(b) - Suo Motu Proactive Disclosure (17 Manuals)**: Every public authority must publish within 120 days of enactment:
  - Particulars of its organization, functions, and duties;
  - Powers and duties of its officers and employees;
  - Procedure followed in decision-making channels, supervision, and accountability;
  - Norms set for the discharge of functions;
  - Rules, regulations, instructions, manuals, and records used by employees;
  - Statement of categories of documents held or under control;
  - Directory of officers and monthly remuneration;
  - Budget allocations, proposed expenditures, and reports on disbursements;
  - Manner of execution of subsidy programmes and beneficiaries;
  - Particulars of concessions, permits, or authorizations granted;
  - Details of information available in electronic form;
  - Names, designations, and particulars of Public Information Officers (PIOs).
- **Section 4(2)**: Continuous endeavor to provide as much information suo motu to the public at regular intervals through various internet and communication channels, minimizing individual recourse to the Act.

---

## 4. Procedure for Requesting Information (Sections 6 & 7)
- **Section 6(1)**: Application in writing or electronic means in English, Hindi, or the official language of the area, accompanied by prescribed fee.
- **Section 6(2) - No Reasons Required**: An applicant making request for information **shall not be required to give any reason** for requesting the information or any other personal details except those necessary for contacting him.
- **Section 6(3) - Transfer of Application**: Where an application or part thereof concerns another public authority, the PIO must transfer it within **5 days** and inform the applicant immediately.
- **Section 7 - Disposal of Requests & Mandatory Timelines**:
  - **General Timeline**: Within **30 days** of receipt of request.
  - **Life or Liberty**: Where information sought concerns the life or liberty of a person, it must be provided within **48 hours** of receipt.
  - **Assistance of Third Party**: 40 days (30 + 10 days under Section 11).
  - **Deemed Refusal**: Failure to give decision within prescribed period is treated as deemed refusal (Section 7(2)).
  - **Free of Cost**: If public authority fails to comply with the time limits, information must be provided free of charge (Section 7(6)).

---

## 5. Exemptions from Disclosure (Sections 8, 9 & 24)
- **Section 8(1) - Ground Exemptions**: Notwithstanding anything contained in the Act, there shall be no obligation to give any citizen:
  - **8(1)(a)**: Information disclosure of which prejudicially affects sovereignty and integrity of India, security, strategic, scientific, or economic interests of the State, relation with foreign State, or leads to incitement of an offence.
  - **8(1)(b)**: Information expressly forbidden to be published by any court of law or tribunal or disclosure which constitutes contempt of court.
  - **8(1)(c)**: Information causing a breach of privilege of Parliament or State Legislature.
  - **8(1)(d)**: Commercial confidence, trade secrets, intellectual property, disclosure of which harms competitive position, unless larger public interest warrants disclosure.
  - **8(1)(e)**: Information available to a person in his fiduciary relationship, unless competent authority is satisfied larger public interest warrants disclosure.
  - **8(1)(f)**: Information received in confidence from foreign Government.
  - **8(1)(g)**: Information endangering life or physical safety of any person, or identifying source of information or assistance given in confidence for law enforcement.
  - **8(1)(h)**: Information impeding the process of investigation or apprehension/prosecution of offenders.
  - **8(1)(i)**: Cabinet papers including records of deliberations of Council of Ministers, Secretaries, and other officers (Proviso: Decisions taken and material on basis of which decisions were taken shall be made public after decision is taken and matter is complete).
  - **8(1)(j)**: Personal information which has no relationship to any public activity or interest, or which causes unwarranted invasion of privacy of the individual (overridden by DPDP Act 2023 amendment to provide blanket privacy protection).
- **Section 8(2) - Public Interest Override**: A public authority may allow access to information if public interest in disclosure outweighs the harm to the protected interests (overrides Official Secrets Act, 1923).
- **Section 8(3) - 20-Year Rule**: Any information relating to an event or matter occurring 20 years before the date of request must be disclosed, except clauses (a), (c), and (i).
- **Section 10 - Severability Clause**: If a request is rejected because it falls under exemptions, access may be provided to that part of the record which does not contain exempt information and can reasonably be severed.
- **Section 24 - Second Schedule Intelligence/Security Exemption**: Intelligence and security organizations specified in Second Schedule (IB, RAW, DRI, ED, NCB, CBI, etc.) are exempt, **except allegations of corruption and human rights violations** (human rights violation disclosures require prior approval of Central Information Commission within 45 days).

## 6. Appellate Structure & Penalties (Sections 19 & 20)
- **First Appeal (Section 19(1))**:
  - To senior officer in rank to PIO (First Appellate Authority - FAA) within **30 days** of expiry of period or receipt of decision.
  - Must be disposed of within **30 days** (extendable to **45 days** for recorded reasons).
- **Second Appeal (Section 19(3))**:
  - To Central Information Commission (CIC) or State Information Commission (SIC) within **90 days**.
  - Decision of CIC/SIC is **binding** (Section 19(7)).
- **Section 20 - Penalties**:
  - **Financial Penalty**: Where PIO without reasonable cause refused, delayed (>30 days), malafidely denied, or gave incorrect/incomplete/misleading information, or destroyed information: **Rs. 250 per day**, up to a maximum of **Rs. 25,000**.
  - **Disciplinary Action**: Recommendation for disciplinary action under applicable service rules for persistent defaults.
  - Burden of proof lies on PIO to prove he acted reasonably and diligently.

---

## 7. Structure of Information Commissions & 2019 Amendment
- **Central Information Commission (Sections 12 & 13)**: Chief Information Commissioner (CIC) and up to 10 Information Commissioners (ICs).
  - Appointed by President on recommendation of Committee consisting of: Prime Minister (Chairperson), Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha, and Union Cabinet Minister nominated by Prime Minister.
- **RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019 Changes**:
  - **Tenure**: Removed fixed 5-year tenure; replaced by tenure "as prescribed by Central Government" (notified as 3 years).
  - **Salaries & Allowances**: Removed statutory parity with Chief Election Commissioner / Election Commissioners; terms, salaries, and service conditions now determined by Central Government rules.
  - **Criticism**: Dilution of autonomy, institutional independence, and federal structure.

## 8. Landmark Judicial Precedents on RTI
1. **Supreme Court of India v. Subhash Chandra Agarwal (2019) (5-Judge Constitution Bench)**:
   - Held: The Office of the Chief Justice of India (CJI) is a "public authority" under Section 2(h) of the RTI Act.
   - Judicial independence and transparency are not mutually exclusive; balance between Article 19(1)(a) right to information and right to privacy/confidentiality.
2. **DAV College Trust and Management Society v. Director of Public Instruction (2019)**:
   - Held: Non-governmental organizations (NGOs) and educational institutions receiving substantial government financing (direct or indirect, including subsidized land) fall within Section 2(h) as public authorities.
3. **Institute of Chartered Accountants of India v. Shaunak H. Satya (2011)**:
   - Held: Fiduciary exemption under Section 8(1)(e) must be construed purposively; examining bodies hold answer sheets and evaluation schemes, but evaluated answer scripts can be inspected by candidates.
4. **CBSE v. Aditya Bandopadhyay (2011)**:
   - Students have the right to inspect their evaluated answer scripts under RTI; examining bodies cannot claim fiduciary exemption to deny examinee access.

## 9. UPSC High-Yield Answer Framing & Keywords
- **Core Principles**: Democratic accountability, sunshine legislation, participatory democracy, citizen-centric administration, proactive governance, institutional autonomy.
- **Key Sections to Quote**:
  - Section 2(h) (Public Authority scope)
  - Section 4(1)(b) (17 manuals of suo motu disclosure)
  - Section 6(2) (No locus standi / no reason required)
  - Section 7(1) (30-day limit; 48 hours for life/liberty)
  - Section 8(1)(j) (Privacy balance)
  - Section 8(2) (Public interest override)
  - Section 20 (Rs. 250/day penalty up to Rs. 25,000)
- **Current Challenges**: Vacancies in CIC/SICs, massive pendency of second appeals, physical harassment/killings of RTI activists (whistleblower vulnerability), weaponization of Section 8(1)(j) post-DPDP Act, dilution through 2019 Amendment.
