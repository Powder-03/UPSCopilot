# The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 (Act No. 1 of 2014)

## 1. Statutory Identity & Legislative Purpose
- **Full Title**: The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013
- **Enactment Date**: Assented to on 1 January 2014; Came into force on 16 January 2014.
- **Nodal Ministry**: Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT), Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions.
- **Historical Background**: First recommended by the First Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC) headed by Morarji Desai (1966) based on the Scandinavian Ombudsman model. Passed following the India Against Corruption (IAC) civil society movement led by Anna Hazare.
- **Preamble & Objective**: An Act to provide for the establishment of a body of Lokpal for the Union and Lokayukta for States to inquire into allegations of corruption against certain public functionaries and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.
- **Constitutional Nexus**:
  - **Articles 14 & 21**: Rule of law, non-arbitrariness in public administration, elimination of systemic corruption in welfare distribution.
  - **Second ARC 4th Report**: "Ethics in Governance" recommending an autonomous anti-corruption ombudsman with investigative teeth.
  - **UN Convention Against Corruption (UNCAC)**: India ratified UNCAC in 2011; Act satisfies international treaty commitments for independent anti-corruption bodies.

---

## 2. Composition & Appointment of Lokpal (Sections 3 & 4)
- **Composition (Section 3)**:
  - **Chairperson**: Either a former Chief Justice of India, a former Judge of the Supreme Court, or an eminent person with flawless integrity and outstanding ability having special knowledge of at least 25 years in anti-corruption policy, public administration, vigilance, finance, or law.
  - **Members**: Up to **8 members**, of which:
    - **50% must be Judicial Members** (former Supreme Court Judges or High Court Chief Justices);
    - **50% from SC, ST, OBC, Minorities, and Women** categories.
- **Selection Committee (Section 4)**:
  - Appointed by the President on the recommendation of a 5-member Selection Committee:
    1. Prime Minister (Chairperson);
    2. Speaker of the House of the People (Lok Sabha);
    3. Leader of Opposition (LoP) in the Lok Sabha (or leader of largest single opposition party as amended);
    4. Chief Justice of India (or a Supreme Court Judge nominated by CJI);
    5. One eminent jurist nominated by the President on the recommendation of the other 4 members.
- **Search Committee (Section 4(3))**: 7-member Search Committee to shortlist panel of candidates; 50% must also be from SC, ST, OBC, Minorities, and Women.

---

## 3. Jurisdiction of Lokpal (Section 14)
- **Extensive Jurisdiction**:
  1. **Prime Minister**:
     - Covered under Lokpal jurisdiction, **except** allegations relating to:
       - International relations;
       - External and internal security;
       - Public order;
       - Atomic energy and space.
     - Special Procedure for PM: Full Bench of Lokpal (Chairperson + all members) must consider complaint; inquiry must be held **in-camera**; at least **two-thirds of members** must approve inquiry; if dismissed, records cannot be made public.
  2. **Ministers & Members of Parliament (MPs)**:
     - All Union Ministers.
     - Members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha) for corruption under Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (PCA).
     - *Article 105(2) Shield*: Lokpal **cannot** inquire into anything said or any vote given by an MP inside Parliament or its committees.
  3. **Government Servants**:
     - All Group A, B, C, and D officers and employees of the Central Government.
  4. **Public & Private Entities**:
     - Chairpersons, members, officers of bodies established by Central Act, or financed/controlled by Central Government.
     - Directors/managers of NGOs or societies receiving government grants exceeding prescribed amount, or foreign donations under FCRA exceeding **Rs. 10 Lakhs** per year.

---

## 4. Investigative & Inquiring Wings (Sections 11, 12 & 20)
- **Inquiry Wing (Section 11)**: Headed by Director of Inquiry for conducting preliminary inquiry.
- **Prosecution Wing (Section 12)**: Headed by Director of Prosecution for conducting prosecution before Special Courts.
- **Preliminary Inquiry Procedure (Section 20)**:
  - Upon receiving a complaint, Lokpal may:
    - Order a preliminary inquiry by its own Inquiry Wing or any agency (including Central Vigilance Commission - CVC, or CBI);
    - Order an immediate investigation by any agency (including CBI) if prima facie case exists.
  - **Superintendence over CBI (Section 25)**: Lokpal has powers of **superintendence and direction over CBI** in respect of matters referred by Lokpal. An investigating officer of CBI investigating a Lokpal case **cannot be transferred without the prior approval of Lokpal**.
  - **Search & Seizure (Section 21)**: Lokpal has powers of civil court and power to authorize search and seizure.
  - **Confiscation of Assets (Section 29)**: Power to provisionally attach and confiscate properties acquired through corrupt means even during pendency of trial.

---

## 5. Timeline for Investigation & Prosecution (Section 20)
- **Preliminary Inquiry**: Must be completed within **90 days** (extendable by 90 days for recorded reasons; total 180 days).
- **Full Investigation**: Must be completed within **6 months** (extendable by 6 months at a time).
- **Trial by Special Courts**: Trial must be completed within **1 year** (extendable up to 2 years max).

---

## 6. Protection of Whistleblowers & Penalties for False Complaints
- **Whistleblower Protection**: Lokpal may issue directions to protect complainants and witnesses from victimization or harassment.
- **Section 46 - Penalty for False and Frivolous Complaints**:
  - Imprisonment up to **1 year** and fine up to **Rs. 1 Lakh** for false, frivolous, or vexatious complaints.
  - (Criticized for creating a chilling effect on genuine whistleblowers).

---

## 7. State Lokayuktas (Part III, Section 63)
- Section 63 mandates that every State shall establish a body known as the **Lokayukta** by state legislation within 1 year of commencement of the Act, if not already established.
- State autonomy preserved: States have freedom to design specific structure, jurisdiction, and appointment methods through state laws (e.g., Karnataka Lokayukta, Maharashtra Lokayukta).

---

## 8. 2016 Amendment to Lokpal Act
- **Public Servants Asset Declaration (Section 44)**:
  - Original Section 44 required public servants to disclose all assets and liabilities, including those of spouse and dependent children, within 30 days of joining and annually, to be published in public domain.
  - **2016 Amendment**: Diluted this requirement; deleted the 30-day timeline and public disclosure format, leaving form and manner of asset disclosure to subordinate rules made by Government.
  - **Leader of Opposition Amendment**: Allowed the leader of the largest single opposition party to participate in the Selection Committee in absence of a recognized Leader of Opposition (>= 10% seats).

---

## 9. Critical Deficiencies & UPSC Answer Points
- **Delay in Operationalization**: First Lokpal (Justice P.C. Ghose) was appointed only in March 2019, more than 5 years after the Act came into force.
- **Lack of Independent Investigative Cadre**: Still heavily reliant on CBI and external police agencies for investigation; lack of dedicated, independent detective staff.
- **Subordinate to Executive Budgets**: Funding and staffing rely on DoPT administrative approvals.
- **Low Public Awareness & Complaint Inflow**: Most complaints received are dismissed at preliminary stage due to lack of standard format or outside jurisdiction.
- **Overlap with CVC & Lokayuktas**: Potential duplication of anti-corruption jurisdiction.

---

## 10. UPSC High-Yield Answer Framing & Keywords
- **Core Pillars**: Institutional independence, Ombudsman accountability, Anti-corruption architecture, Superintendence over CBI, Tripartite integrity system (Lokpal + CVC + CBI).
- **High-Yield Concepts**:
  - Scandinavia ombudsman lineage (1809 Swedish Justitieombudsman)
  - 1st ARC (1966) & 2nd ARC (2007) Ethics in Governance
  - Prime Minister's guarded inclusion (Section 14(1)(a) in-camera & 2/3rd majority)
  - Section 25 power of superintendence over CBI
  - Section 44 asset declaration dilution under 2016 amendment.
