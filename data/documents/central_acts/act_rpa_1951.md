# Representation of the People Act, 1951 (Act No. 43 of 1951)

## 1. Statutory Identity & Legislative Purpose
- **Full Title**: The Representation of the People Act, 1951
- **Enactment Date**: 17 July 1951 (Pre-First General Elections of independent India).
- **Nodal Agency**: Election Commission of India (ECI) / Ministry of Law and Justice (Legislative Department).
- **Distinction between RPA 1950 and RPA 1951**:
  - **RPA 1950**: Governs allocation of seats in House of the People and Legislative Assemblies, delimitation of constituencies, qualifications of voters, preparation of electoral rolls (Sections 14-25A), and Chief Electoral Officer (CEO) / District Election Officer (DEO) statutory positions.
  - **RPA 1951**: Governs the actual conduct of elections, qualifications and disqualifications for membership of Parliament and State Legislatures, corrupt practices, electoral offences, registration of political parties, election disputes, and model code statutory nexus.
- **Constitutional Nexus**:
  - **Article 324**: Superintendence, direction, and control of elections vested in the Election Commission.
  - **Article 325 & 326**: Universal adult suffrage; non-discrimination in electoral rolls.
  - **Article 327 & 328**: Power of Parliament and State Legislatures to make laws regarding elections.
  - **Article 102 & 191**: Constitutional disqualifications for MPs and MLAs, supplemented by statutory disqualifications under RPA 1951.

---

## 2. Qualifications for Membership (Sections 3 to 6)
- **Section 3 - Rajya Sabha Qualifications**: Must be a citizen of India, at least 30 years old, and an elector for any parliamentary constituency in India. (Requirement of being an elector of that specific State was deleted by 2003 amendment; upheld in *Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India*, 2006).
- **Section 4 - Lok Sabha Qualifications**: Must be an elector for any parliamentary constituency in India, at least 25 years old. In reserved seats for Scheduled Castes or Scheduled Tribes, must belong to that community.
- **Section 5 - Legislative Assembly (SLA)**: Elector for any assembly constituency in that State, at least 25 years old.
- **Section 6 - Legislative Council (SLC)**: Elector in that State, at least 30 years old.

---

## 3. Disqualifications for Membership (Sections 8 to 11A)
- **Section 8 - Disqualification on Conviction for Offences**:
  - **Section 8(1)**: Disqualification upon conviction for specific offences regardless of sentence duration (e.g., promoting enmity between groups u/s 153A IPC, bribery, rape, untouchability under Protection of Civil Rights Act, prevention of corruption, customs violation, terrorism under UAPA). Disqualified from date of conviction and continues for **6 years since release**.
  - **Section 8(2)**: Conviction for contravention of laws relating to hoarding, profiteering, food adulteration, dowry, with imprisonment for not less than **6 months**; disqualified for 6 years post-release.
  - **Section 8(3) - Blanket Conviction Rule**: A person convicted of **any other offence** and sentenced to imprisonment for **not less than 2 years** shall be disqualified from the date of conviction and shall continue to be disqualified for a further period of **6 years since release**.
  - **Section 8(4) - Struck Down in *Lily Thomas* (2013)**:
    - Originally gave a 3-month protection window for sitting MPs/MLAs to appeal without immediate disqualification.
    - Struck down as ultra vires Article 102(1)(e) and 191(1)(e) in *Lily Thomas v. Union of India* (2013); disqualification is now **instantaneous** upon sentencing of >= 2 years.
- **Section 8A**: Disqualification on ground of corrupt practices (referred to President, who decides after obtaining opinion of Election Commission).
- **Section 9**: Disqualification for dismissal from government service for corruption or disloyalty to the State (5 years).
- **Section 9A**: Disqualification for government contracts (holding a subsisting contract with the appropriate Government for supply of goods or execution of works).
- **Section 10**: Disqualification for office under government company (holding office of director/manager in company with >= 25% government equity).
- **Section 10A - Failure to Lodge Election Expenses**: Disqualification for failure to lodge an account of election expenses within the time and manner required, without good reason (disqualification up to 3 years by ECI; e.g., *Umlesh Yadav case* on paid news).

## 4. Registration & Regulation of Political Parties (Section 29A, 29B, 29C)
- **Section 29A - Registration with ECI**:
  - Application to Election Commission within 30 days of formation.
  - Mandatory constitutional pledge: Party constitution must contain a specific provision pledging true faith and allegiance to the Constitution of India, principles of socialism, secularism, and democracy, and upholding sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India.
  - Note: ECI has the power to register political parties under Section 29A, but **does not have statutory power to de-register political parties** for violating election norms (*Indian National Congress v. Institute of Social Welfare*, 2002), except for fraud in registration or declared unconstitutional/unlawful by Central Government.
- **Section 29B & 29C - Political Donations & Reporting**:
  - Political parties may accept voluntary contributions from individuals and domestic companies (excluding government companies and foreign sources as per Foreign Contribution Regulation Act - FCRA).
  - **Section 29C - Contribution Reports**: Treasurer of party must prepare report of all donations exceeding **Rs. 20,000** received from individuals or companies and submit to ECI annually before due date of income tax return (failure forfeits tax exemptions under Section 13A of Income Tax Act).
  - Electoral Bonds Scheme (2018) amended Section 29C to exempt bonds from disclosure; this was declared unconstitutional and struck down by 5-Judge Bench in *Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India* (2024).

## 5. Corrupt Practices vs. Electoral Offences (Sections 123 & 125 to 136)
- **Section 123 - Corrupt Practices (Grounds to Declare Election Void u/s 100)**:
  - **123(1) - Bribery**: Direct or indirect gift, offer, or promise of gratification to induce voting.
  - **123(2) - Undue Influence**: Direct or indirect interference or attempt to interfere with the free exercise of electoral right (threat of injury, excommunication, divine displeasure).
  - **123(3) - Appeals on Religion, Race, Caste, Community, or Language**:
    - Extended by 7-Judge Bench in *Abhiram Singh v. C.D. Commachen* (2017) to interpret "his religion" purposively to include the religion of the candidate, rival candidate, election agent, or the voters (secular ethos of elections).
  - **123(3A)**: Promotion of feelings of enmity or hatred between different classes of citizens on grounds of religion, race, caste, community.
  - **123(4)**: Publication of false statements in relation to personal character or conduct of any candidate.
  - **123(6)**: Incurring election expenditure in excess of limits prescribed under Section 77 (Rule 90 of Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961).
  - **123(7)**: Procuring or obtaining assistance of government servants (gazetted officers, police, armed forces, revenue officials) for furthering election prospects (*State of UP v. Raj Narain*, 1975).
  - **123(8) - Booth Capturing**: Seizing polling stations, intimidating voters, physical takeover of EVMs.
- **Key Electoral Offences (Part VII, Sections 125 to 136)**:
  - Section 126: Prohibition of public meetings and political broadcasts during period of **48 hours ending with hour fixed for conclusion of poll** (Silent Period).
  - Section 128: Maintenance of secrecy of voting.
  - Section 133: Illegal hiring or procuring of conveyances at elections.
  - Section 135B: Grant of paid holiday to employees on the day of poll.

## 6. Election Disputes & Election Petitions (Part VI, Sections 80 to 116C)
- **Article 329(b) Bar**: No election to either House of Parliament or State Legislature shall be called in question except by an **Election Petition**.
- **Section 80A - High Court Jurisdiction**: Election petitions are heard directly by the **High Court** within whose jurisdiction the election took place.
- **Section 81 - Limitation**: Must be presented within **45 days** from the date of election of the returned candidate.
- **Section 100 - Grounds for Declaring Election Void**:
  - Candidate was not qualified or was disqualified on election date;
  - Any corrupt practice was committed by returned candidate or election agent;
  - Improper acceptance or improper rejection of nomination paper;
  - Non-compliance with Constitution or provisions of RPA 1951.
- **Section 116A - Appeal**: Appeals from High Court orders lie directly to the Supreme Court of India.

## 7. Landmark Judicial Rulings Interpreting RPA
1. **Union of India v. Association for Democratic Reforms (ADR) (2002)**:
   - Mandatory criminal antecedent, educational qualification, and asset/liability disclosures in Form 26 affidavits under Article 19(1)(a) voter's right to know.
2. **People's Union for Civil Liberties (PUCL) v. Union of India (2013)**:
   - Directed introduction of **NOTA (None of the Above)** option on EVMs under Rule 49-O to ensure secret right of negative voting.
3. **Subramanian Swamy v. Election Commission of India (2013)**:
   - Mandated phased introduction of **VVPAT (Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail)** to ensure transparency and paper verification of EVM votes.
4. **Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013)**:
   - Struck down Section 8(4); sitting lawmakers disqualified instantaneously upon conviction of >= 2 years.
5. **Lok Prahari v. Union of India (2018)**:
   - Fast-tracking of trial of MPs/MLAs; stay on conviction (not just suspension of sentence u/s 389 CrPC) required to reverse Section 8 disqualification.
6. **Public Interest Foundation v. Union of India (2018)**:
   - Directed political parties and candidates to publish criminal antecedents in newspapers, TV channels, and official websites at least 3 times during campaign.
