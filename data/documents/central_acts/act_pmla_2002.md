# Prevention of Money-Laundering Act, 2002 (Act No. 15 of 2003)

## 1. Statutory Identity & Legislative Purpose
- **Full Title**: The Prevention of Money-Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA)
- **Enactment Date**: Enacted in 2002; came into force on **1 July 2005**.
- **Nodal Agency**: **Directorate of Enforcement (ED)**, Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance.
- **International Commitments & Mandate**:
  - Enacted to fulfill India's international obligations under:
    - UN General Assembly Political Declaration & Global Programme of Action (1990);
    - UN Convention Against Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (Vienna Convention, 1988);
    - Financial Action Task Force (FATF) 40 Recommendations on Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Combating Financing of Terrorism (CFT);
    - UN Convention Against Transnational Organized Crime (Palermo Convention, 2000).
- **Preamble & Objective**: An Act to prevent money-laundering and to provide for confiscation of property derived from, or involved in, money-laundering and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.
- **Constitutional Nexus**:
  - **Articles 20 & 21**: Protection against self-incrimination, presumption of innocence, procedure established by law, personal liberty vs. economic security of the State.
  - **Schedule 7, List I, Entry 93**: Offences against laws with respect to any of the matters in List I.

---

## 2. Key Statutory Definitions & The Offence (Sections 2 & 3)
- **Section 2(1)(u) - "Proceeds of Crime"**:
  - Any property derived or obtained, directly or indirectly, by any person as a result of criminal activity relating to a **scheduled offence**;
  - Or the value of any such property;
  - Or where such property is taken or held outside the country, then property equivalent in value held within the country or abroad.
  - **Explanation added by 2019 Amendment**: "Proceeds of crime" includes property not only derived from scheduled offence, but any property which may directly or indirectly be derived or obtained as a result of any criminal activity relatable to the scheduled offence.
- **Section 2(1)(y) - "Scheduled Offence" (Predicate Offence)**:
  - Offences specified under Part A, Part B, and Part C of the Schedule to PMLA (e.g., offences under IPC/BNS like fraud, cheating, murder, kidnapping, corruption under PCA, drug trafficking under NDPS, terrorism under UAPA, wildlife trafficking).
  - PMLA is not an independent stand-alone offence in vacuum; it requires a **predicate/scheduled offence** from which proceeds of crime originated.
- **Section 3 - Offence of Money-Laundering**:
  - Whosoever directly or indirectly attempts to indulge, knowingly assists, is a party to, or is actually involved in any process or activity connected with the proceeds of crime including its:
    - **Concealment**;
    - **Possession**;
    - **Acquisition**;
    - **Use**;
    - **Projecting as untainted property**; or
    - **Claiming as untainted property**.
  - **2019 Clarification**: The process or activity connected with proceeds of crime is a **continuing activity** and continues so long as a person is enjoying the proceeds of crime by its concealment, possession, acquisition, use, or projecting/claiming it as untainted.

## 3. Attachment, Adjudication & Confiscation (Sections 5 & 8)
- **Section 5 - Provisional Attachment**:
  - Director or officer not below rank of Deputy Director may, by order in writing, **provisionally attach** property for a period not exceeding **180 days** if there is reason to believe (recorded in writing) that person is in possession of proceeds of crime and such proceeds are likely to be concealed, transferred, or dealt with.
  - Proviso: Prior FIR/charge sheet in scheduled offence is generally required, unless delay would frustrate proceedings under the Act.
- **Section 6 - Adjudicating Authority**:
  - 3-member independent body appointed by Central Government (legal, financial, administrative experts) to adjudicate attachments.
- **Section 8 - Confirmation of Attachment**:
  - Adjudicating Authority issues show-cause notice (30 days);
  - Hears parties and passes order confirming or releasing attachment;
  - On confirmation, attachment continues during trial before Special Court;
  - On conclusion of trial, if accused is convicted, property stands **confiscated to Central Government**.

---

## 4. Special Powers of Enforcement Directorate (ED)
- **Section 17 - Search and Seizure**: Power to enter, search building, break open lockers, seize records and property where officer has reason to believe offence of money laundering has occurred. (Requirement of prior FIR/charge sheet was removed by 2019 amendment).
- **Section 19 - Power of Arrest**:
  - Director, Deputy Director, or Assistant Director may arrest any person if they have "reason to believe" (reason recorded in writing) that such person is guilty of an offence punishable under PMLA.
  - Must inform the arrested person of the grounds of arrest "as soon as may be";
  - Must produce the arrested person before a Judicial/Special Magistrate within **24 hours**.
- **Section 50 - Summons, Production of Documents & Admissibility of Statements**:
  - ED officers have powers of a civil court to summon any person and examine them on oath.
  - **Non-applicability of Section 25 of Indian Evidence Act / Article 20(3)**:
    - Statements recorded by ED officers under Section 50 are **admissible as evidence in court**.
    - ED officers are **not considered "police officers"**; hence statements made to them are not hit by Section 25 of the Evidence Act (which bars confessions made to police).
- **Enforcement Case Information Report (ECIR)**:
  - Internal administrative document of ED (equivalent to police FIR); ED is **not legally bound to provide a copy of ECIR to the accused** at the time of arrest; communicating grounds of arrest in writing or verbally suffices (*Vijay Madanlal Choudhary*).

---

## 5. Twin Conditions for Bail (Section 45)
- **Section 45(1) - Rigorous Bail Conditions**:
  - No person accused of an offence under PMLA shall be released on bail unless:
    1. The Public Prosecutor has been given an opportunity to oppose the application for bail; and
    2. Where the Public Prosecutor opposes the application, the Court is satisfied that there are **reasonable grounds for believing that the accused is not guilty of such offence** and that **he is not likely to commit any offence while on bail**.
  - Shifts the burden of proof at the pre-trial bail stage onto the accused.
  - **Exceptions**: Person under 16 years of age, woman, sick, or infirm may be released on bail by Special Court.
- **Judicial History of Section 45**:
  - In *Nikesh Tarachand Shah v. Union of India* (2018), the Supreme Court struck down twin bail conditions as unconstitutional (violative of Articles 14 and 21).
  - Parliament amended Section 45 in 2018 to cure the defect.
  - In *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary (2022)*, the Supreme Court **upheld the amended twin conditions as constitutional**, citing money laundering as a heinous economic menace against the integrity of the financial system.

---

## 6. Reverse Burden of Proof (Section 24)
- In any proceedings relating to proceeds of crime:
  - In the case of a person charged with the offence of money-laundering, the Authority or Court **shall presume** that such proceeds of crime are involved in money-laundering (reverses standard presumption of innocence in criminal jurisprudence).
  - The legal burden rests on the accused to prove that property claimed is from untainted, lawful sources.

---

## 7. Landmark Judicial Decisions on PMLA
1. **Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India (2022) (3-Judge Bench)**:
   - Upheld constitutionality of PMLA core provisions:
     - Upheld wide definition of "proceeds of crime" under Section 3;
     - Upheld Section 5 provisional attachment before charge sheet;
     - Upheld Section 19 arrest powers;
     - Upheld admissibility of Section 50 statements made to ED officers (ED is not police);
     - Upheld Section 45 twin bail conditions;
     - Held ECIR is an internal document, not identical to FIR; not mandatory to furnish copy of ECIR to accused so long as grounds of arrest are informed.
2. **Pankaj Bansal v. Union of India (2023)**:
   - Clarified that communication of "grounds of arrest" under Section 19 must be **in writing to the accused** to satisfy constitutional mandate of Article 22(1).
3. **Pavana Dibbur v. Directorate of Enforcement (2023)**:
   - Held: If there is no scheduled/predicate offence committed, there can be no proceeds of crime, and consequently no offence under Section 3 PMLA. Discharge or acquittal in predicate offence automatically terminates PMLA prosecution.
4. **Manish Sisodia v. Directorate of Enforcement (2024)**:
   - Held: Right to speedy trial under Article 21 is a higher fundamental right; protracted incarceration without trial overrides Section 45 twin bail conditions.

## 8. UPSC High-Yield Answer Framing & Keywords
- **Core Pillars**: Financial integrity, FATF compliance, Illicit financial flows, Hawala transactions, Shell companies, Sovereign economic security.
- **High-Yield Concepts**:
  - Predicate / Scheduled offence dependency (Section 2(1)(y))
  - Section 3 six-fold nexus (concealment, possession, acquisition, use, projecting, claiming)
  - Section 24 reverse burden of proof
  - Section 45 twin conditions (*Nikesh Tarachand Shah* vs. *Vijay Madanlal*)
  - Section 50 non-police status of ED officers vs. Article 20(3)
  - Federalism concerns: PMLA enforcement on state political leadership without state consent (unlike Section 6 of DSPE Act which requires state general consent for CBI).
