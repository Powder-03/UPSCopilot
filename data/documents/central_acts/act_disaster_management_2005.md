# Disaster Management Act, 2005 (Act No. 53 of 2005)

## 1. Statutory Identity & Legislative Purpose
- **Full Title**: The Disaster Management Act, 2005
- **Enactment Date**: 26 December 2005 (Enacted in the aftermath of the devastating 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami).
- **Nodal Ministry**: Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA), Government of India.
- **Preamble & Objective**: An Act to provide for the effective management of disasters and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.
- **Paradigm Shift in India's Disaster Policy**:
  - Shifted India's approach from a reactive, post-disaster relief and rescue model to a **proactive, holistic, multi-hazard, institutional model** focusing on prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response, recovery, and rehabilitation.
- **Constitutional Nexus**:
  - Entry 23 (Social security and insurance; employment and unemployment) and Entry 29 (Prevention of extension from one State to another of infectious or contagious diseases) of Concurrent List (List III), alongside Union List residuary powers (Article 248).
  - **Article 21**: Right to life encompasses State's positive obligation to protect citizens from preventable disasters and provide minimum standards of relief.

---

## 2. Key Statutory Definitions (Section 2)
- **Section 2(d) - "Disaster"**:
  - A catastrophe, mishap, calamity or grave occurrence in any area, arising from **natural or man-made causes**, or by accident or negligence;
  - Which results in:
    - Substantial loss of life or human suffering; or
    - Damage to, and destruction of, property; or
    - Damage to, or degradation of, environment;
  - And is of such a nature or magnitude as to be **beyond the coping capacity of the community of the affected area**.
- **Section 2(e) - "Disaster Management"**:
  - A continuous and integrated process of planning, organizing, coordinating, and implementing measures necessary or expedient for:
    - Prevention of danger or threat of any disaster;
    - Mitigation or reduction of risk of any disaster or its severity;
    - Capacity-building;
    - Preparedness to deal with any disaster;
    - Prompt response to any threatening disaster situation or disaster;
    - Assessing the severity or magnitude of effects of any disaster;
    - Evacuation, rescue, and relief;
    - Rehabilitation and reconstruction.

## 3. Three-Tier Institutional Architecture (National, State, District)

### A. National Level (NDMA & NEC)
- **National Disaster Management Authority - NDMA (Section 3)**:
  - Apex body for disaster management in India.
  - **Chairperson**: **Prime Minister of India** (ex officio).
  - Members: Up to **9 members** nominated by the Prime Minister; one designated as Vice-Chairperson (Cabinet Minister rank).
  - **Powers & Functions (Section 6)**:
    - Lay down policies on disaster management;
    - Approve the National Plan;
    - Approve plans prepared by Ministries and Departments of Government of India;
    - Lay down guidelines to be followed by State Authorities in drawing up State Plan;
    - Coordinate enforcement and implementation of policy and plans;
    - Recommend provision of funds for mitigation.
- **National Executive Committee - NEC (Section 8)**:
  - Executive committee assisting NDMA in discharge of its functions.
  - **Chairperson**: **Union Home Secretary** (ex officio).
  - Responsible for preparing the National Disaster Management Plan, monitoring implementation, and issuing binding executive directions.

### B. State Level (SDMA & SEC)
- **State Disaster Management Authority - SDMA (Section 14)**:
  - **Chairperson**: **Chief Minister of the State** (ex officio).
  - Members: Up to 8 members nominated by Chief Minister.
  - Lays down state disaster management policies, approves State Disaster Management Plan, and coordinates district plans.
- **State Executive Committee - SEC (Section 20)**:
  - Headed by the **Chief Secretary of the State** (ex officio).

### C. District Level (DDMA - The Operational Fulcrum)
- **District Disaster Management Authority - DDMA (Section 25)**:
  - Core operational unit and planning authority at the grassroots level.
  - **Chairperson**: **District Magistrate (DM) / Collector / Deputy Commissioner** (ex officio).
  - **Co-Chairperson**: Elected representative of local authority (e.g., Zilla Parishad President).
  - **Chief Executive Officer (CEO)**: An officer not below the rank of Additional District Magistrate.
  - **Powers (Section 30)**:
    - Prepares District Disaster Management Plan;
    - Coordinates and monitors implementation of National, State, and District plans;
    - Identifies vulnerable areas, ensures warning systems work;
    - Directs any department at district level to take disaster prevention/response measures;
    - Controls movement of persons and vehicles in disaster zones.

## 4. Specialized Enforcement & Operational Arms

### A. National Disaster Response Force - NDRF (Sections 44 & 45)
- Dedicated specialist force constituted for the purpose of specialist response to threatening disaster situations or disasters.
- Headed by a **Director General of NDRF** (DG rank IPS officer) appointed by Central Government.
- Superintended and directed by NDMA; operates in multi-skilled battalions (trained for flood rescue, collapsed structure search and rescue - CSSR, CBRN - chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear emergencies).

### B. National Institute of Disaster Management - NIDM (Sections 42 & 43)
- Apex national institute for capacity building, human resource development, training, research, documentation, and policy advocacy in disaster risk reduction.

---

## 5. Financial Architecture (Sections 46 to 49)
- **National Disaster Response Fund (NDRF)**: Managed by Central Government for meeting expenses for emergency response, relief, and rehabilitation.
- **National Disaster Mitigation Fund (NDMF)**: Created specifically for funding disaster risk reduction and mitigation projects (recommended by 15th Finance Commission).
- **State Disaster Response Fund (SDRF) & State Disaster Mitigation Fund (SDMF)**: State-level counterpart funds funded through Centre-State cost-sharing formulas (90:10 for Himalayan/NE States; 75:25 for other States).
- **District Disaster Response Fund (DDRF) & District Disaster Mitigation Fund (DDMF)**: Operational funds at the district level.

---

## 6. Emergency Executive Powers & Pandemic Application (Section 6 to 10, Section 35, Section 62)
- **Nationwide Application during COVID-19 (2020)**:
  - For the first time in Indian history, the Disaster Management Act, 2005 was invoked nationwide on **24 March 2020** to declare the COVID-19 pandemic as a "notified disaster".
  - **Section 6(2)(i) & Section 10(2)(l)**: National Executive Committee (NEC) headed by Union Home Secretary issued binding nationwide lockdown guidelines, travel restrictions, mask mandates, and hospital oxygen allocation protocols.
  - **Section 62 - Power to Give Directions**: Central Government may give directions to any State Government or authority, and such directions are **statutorily binding**.
  - **Federalism Tension**: While "Public Health and Sanitation" is Entry 6 of the State List (List II), Centre utilized the DM Act to enforce a centralized command structure overriding state autonomy.

---

## 7. Offences & Penalties (Sections 51 to 60)
- **Section 51 - Obstruction and Non-Compliance**:
  - Obstructing any officer in discharge of functions, or refusing to comply with directions: Imprisonment up to **1 year**, or fine, or both. If obstruction causes loss of life, imprisonment up to **2 years**.
- **Section 52 - False Claim**: Making false claim to obtain disaster relief or assistance: Imprisonment up to **2 years** and fine.
- **Section 53 - Misappropriation of Money or Materials**: Misappropriation of relief funds or materials: Imprisonment up to **2 years** and fine.
- **Section 54 - False Alarm / Rumour Mongering**: Making or circulating a false alarm or warning as to disaster: Imprisonment up to **1 year** or fine (heavily invoked during COVID-19 infodemic).
- **Section 55**: Offences by Government Departments (Head of Department deemed guilty unless proved offence committed without knowledge).
- **Section 56**: Failure of designated officer in duty or connivance at contravention: Imprisonment up to 1 year or fine.

## 8. Landmark Supreme Court Rulings on DM Act
1. **Swaraj Abhiyan (I) & (II) v. Union of India (2016)**:
   - Supreme Court issued directions to operationalize Section 12 (Minimum standards of relief) and Section 46 (National Disaster Mitigation Fund) for rural drought management and food security under National Food Security Act (NFSA).
2. **Gaurav Kumar Bansal & Reepak Kansal v. Union of India (2021)**:
   - Supreme Court held that ex-gratia assistance to families of persons who died due to COVID-19 is a **statutory obligation** under Section 12(iii) of the DM Act; directed NDMA to recommend uniform ex-gratia compensation of Rs. 50,000 per deceased person.
