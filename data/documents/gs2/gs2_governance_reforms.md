# GS-2: Governance Reforms, Accountability & Institutional Architecture (Canonical Dossier)

## 1. The 2nd Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC) Key Reports

Constituted in 2005 under the chairmanship of Veerappa Moily (later V. Ramachandran) to provide an authoritative blueprint for revamping public administration in India.

### 1st Report: "Right to Information: Master Key to Good Governance"
- **Repeal of the Official Secrets Act, 1923 (OSA)**:
  - Recommended the complete repeal of the draconian colonial Official Secrets Act, 1923, and its substitution by a dedicated chapter on official secrets and national security in the National Security Act.
  - Noted that Section 5 of the OSA operates as an engine of administrative opacity, in direct philosophical conflict with Section 22 of the RTI Act 2005 (which gives the RTI Act overriding effect over all inconsistent laws).
- **Proactive Suo Motu Disclosures (Section 4)**:
  - Every public authority must allocate at least 1% of its operating budget toward computerization, indexing, and regular online updating of Section 4 manuals.
  - Independent monitoring of proactive disclosure by Central and State Information Commissions.
- **Office of Information Commissioners**:
  - Selection Committee should be broad-based, and appointments must be drawn from diverse fields (law, science, social service, journalism), ending the near-monopoly of retired civil servants.

### 6th Report: "Local Governance: An Inspiring Journey into the Future"
- **The "3Fs" Devolution Paradigm**:
  - Genuine empowerment of Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs) under the 73rd Amendment and Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) under the 74th Amendment requires concurrent devolution of:
    1. **Functions**: Clear, unambiguous activity mapping demarcating exclusive functional domains for Gram, Block, and District Panchayats.
    2. **Funds**: State Finance Commissions (SFCs) must be constituted on schedule; state governments must devolve predictable tax-sharing and untied grants.
    3. **Functionaries**: Administrative cadre under the direct operational control, disciplinary oversight, and performance appraisal of elected local bodies.
- **District Planning Committees (Article 243ZD)**:
  - Must be operationalized in every district to consolidate draft development plans prepared by Panchayats and Municipalities into a unified District Development Plan, ending parallel administrative line-department silos.
- **Local Ombudsman**: Establish a separate statutory Ombudsman at the district level to investigate corruption and maladministration in local self-governments.

### 12th Report: "Citizen-Centric Administration: The Heart of Governance"
- **Core Doctrine: The Citizen as Sovereign**:
  - Shifts administrative paradigm from bureaucratic patronage to **"Citizens as Customers and Rights-Holders"**.
- **Enforceable Citizen's Charters**:
  - Every department must formulate a concise Citizen's Charter specifying measurable service delivery standards, process timelines, and remedy mechanisms.
  - Charters must stipulate **monetary compensation for delayed services** to citizens, deducted from the salary of the defaulting public official.
- **Public Grievance Redressal Architecture**:
  - Creation of an independent, statutory **Public Grievance Redressal Authority**; mandatory integration of digital grievance portals (CPGRAMS) with escalating accountability algorithms.

## 2. Citizen's Charters & The Sevottam Framework

### The 6 Core Principles of Citizen's Charters
Originating in the United Kingdom (1991), adopted in India (1997):
1. **Quality**: Improving the quality of public services.
2. **Choice**: Providing choice to users wherever feasible.
3. **Standards**: Specifying explicit, quantifiable service standards and time limits.
4. **Value**: Delivering value for taxpayer money.
5. **Accountability**: Individuals and organizations made accountable for performance.
6. **Transparency**: Openness in rules, procedures, schemes, and grievance mechanisms.

### Critical Deficiencies in Indian Implementation (DARPG Audits)
- Lack of legal enforceability (charters remain pious administrative declarations without statutory force).
- Non-consultative "top-down" formulation without engaging citizens, civil society, or field-level staff.
- Vague, generic commitments (*"We strive to serve promptly"*) rather than quantifiable parameters (*"Birth certificates issued within 7 days"*).
- Absence of compensatory redressal mechanisms for service delivery failure.

### The Sevottam Model (Bureau of Indian Standards IS 15700:2005)
A quality management framework designed by the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG) to elevate service delivery standards across public organizations:

```
                          THE SEVOTTAM MODEL
                                  │
    ┌─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┐
    ▼                             ▼                             ▼
PILLAR 1:                      PILLAR 2:                     PILLAR 3:
Citizen's Charter              Public Grievance              Service Delivery
Implementation                 Redressal                     Capability
  • Clear standards              • Receipt & Acknowledgment    • Infrastructure & ICT
  • Time-bound commitments      • Objective Investigation     • Trained frontline staff
  • Monitoring mechanisms       • Timely Resolution & Closure • Continuous feedback loops
```

1. **Pillar 1: Citizen's Charter**: Verifies that the organization has formulated a realistic charter, publicly displayed it, and actively tracks compliance against declared benchmarks.
2. **Pillar 2: Public Grievance Redressal**: Measures whether grievances are logged with unique tracking IDs, acknowledged within 3 days, redressed within a maximum of 30 days, and analyzed root-cause to prevent recurrence.
3. **Pillar 3: Service Delivery Capability**: Evaluates internal organizational capacity (skilled workforce, citizen-friendly front offices, digital infrastructure) necessary to honor charter commitments.

## 3. Social Audit Framework & Civil Society Oversight

### Concept & Statutory Mandate
- **Definition**: A continuous, participatory process through which the beneficiaries and stakeholders of a government scheme jointly review and verify project records, muster rolls, financial expenditures, and on-ground physical assets.
- **Section 17, MGNREGA Act 2005**:
  - The first central statute in India to mandate **compulsory periodic social audits**.
  - Mandates the **Gram Sabha** to conduct regular social audits of all public works executed within the Gram Panchayat.
  - Requires the program officer to provide all muster rolls, bills, vouchers, sanction orders, and measurement books to the Gram Sabha at least 15 days prior to the audit.
- **Institutionalization: Social Audit Units (SAUs)**:
  - Autonomous directorates independent of the implementing government department, staffed by trained social audit experts and community resource persons (often drawn from Women's Self-Help Groups).
  - Open public hearings (*Jan Sunwai*) held in the village with mandatory attendance of implementing engineers, bank officials, and beneficiaries.

### The Meghalaya Model: Pioneer State Legislation
- **Meghalaya Community Participation and Public Services Social Audit Act, 2017**:
  - First state in India to pass an overarching, dedicated law institutionalizing social audits across **26 government departments and all major welfare schemes** (PDS, Health, Education, ICDS, Roads), expanding social audit beyond MGNREGA into systemic administrative governance.

## 4. NITI Aayog Governance Frameworks

### The Aspirational Districts Programme (ADP, Launched 2018)
- Targeted transformation of the **112 most socio-economically backward districts** across 28 states, home to over 250 million people.
- **The "3Cs" Implementation Philosophy**:
  1. **Convergence**: Harmonizing Central and State Government development schemes without creating new budgetary outlays.
  2. **Collaboration**: Institutional synergy between Central Prabhari Officers (Joint Secretary/Additional Secretary rank), State Nodal Officers, and District Collectors.
  3. **Competition**: Dynamic monthly ranking (*Delta Ranking*) based on real-time data publicly tracked on the Champions of Change dashboard, fostering competitive federalism at the district level.
- **Five Core Focus Sectors & Weightage (49 Key Performance Indicators)**:
  1. *Health & Nutrition* (30% weightage): Antenatal care, institutional deliveries, infant immunizations, child stunting/wasting reduction.
  2. *Education* (30% weightage): Learning outcomes, female pupil-teacher ratios, girl's toilet access.
  3. *Agriculture & Water Resources* (20% weightage): Soil health cards, micro-irrigation coverage, crop insurance.
  4. *Financial Inclusion & Skill Development* (10% weightage): Mudra loans, PM Jan Dhan accounts, Atal Pension Yojana.
  5. *Basic Infrastructure* (10% weightage): Piped drinking water, rural electrification, PMGSY road connectivity.
- **Expansion (2023)**: **Aspirational Blocks Programme (ABP)** launched to replicate this success across **500 underdeveloped administrative blocks** within 329 districts.

### Good Governance Index (GGI)
- Published biennially by DARPG and the Centre for Good Governance.
- Assesses administrative efficiency across 10 sectors (Agriculture, Commerce & Industry, Human Resource, Public Health, Infrastructure, Economic Governance, Social Welfare, Judiciary & Public Safety, Environment, Citizen-Centric Governance) across 4 categories: Other States Group A, Other States Group B, North-East & Hill States, and Union Territories.
