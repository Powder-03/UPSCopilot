# GS-2: International Relations & Strategic Foreign Policy (Canonical Dossier)

## 1. Foundational Diplomatic Doctrines & Strategic Evolution

### The Panchsheel Principles (1954)
Formally articulated in the Sino-Indian Agreement on Tibet (29 April 1954) by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru and Premier Zhou Enlai:
1. Mutual respect for each other's territorial integrity and sovereignty.
2. Mutual non-aggression.
3. Mutual non-interference in each other's internal affairs.
4. Equality and mutual benefit.
5. Peaceful co-existence.

### Evolution: Non-Alignment to Strategic Autonomy & Multi-Alignment
- **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM, Belgrade 1961)**:
  - Formulated by Nehru, Tito, Nasser, Sukarno, and Nkrumah during the Cold War.
  - Refused to align with either the US-led Western bloc or the Soviet-led Eastern bloc; aimed to maintain independent decision-making and support anti-colonial struggles.
- **Modern Multi-Alignment (*Vishwa Mitra*)**:
  - India has transitioned from passive non-alignment to active, issue-based **Strategic Autonomy and Multi-Alignment**.
  - Engages simultaneously across competing geopolitical architectures based on national interest: Member of the Western-leaning **Quad** (India, US, Japan, Australia) while simultaneously engaging in **BRICS** and **SCO** alongside Russia and China.

### The Gujral Doctrine (1996)
Enunciated by External Affairs Minister (later Prime Minister) I.K. Gujral as a landmark framework for South Asian neighborhood diplomacy:
1. With five neighbors (Bangladesh, Bhutan, Maldives, Nepal, Sri Lanka), India **does not ask for reciprocity**, but gives all that it can in good faith and trust.
2. No South Asian country should allow its territory to be used against the interest of another country in the region.
3. Non-interference in the internal affairs of other states.
4. Respect for each other's territorial integrity and sovereignty.
5. Settle all bilateral disputes through peaceful, direct bilateral negotiations.

### Contemporary Regional Doctrines
- **Neighborhood First Policy**: Priority infrastructure financing, line of credits, humanitarian assistance, and transit connectivity to immediate South Asian neighbors.
- **Act East Policy (Upgraded from Look East, 2014)**: Focuses on the **3Cs (Commerce, Connectivity, and Culture)** with ASEAN and the broader Indo-Pacific. Key connectivity projects: Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Transport Project (connecting Kolkata to Sittwe port in Myanmar and Mizoram) and the India-Myanmar-Thailand Trilateral Highway.
- **SAGAR (Security and Growth for All in the Region, 2015)**: Maritime doctrine for the Indian Ocean Region focusing on capacity building, coastal radar networks, Disaster Relief (HADR), and maritime domain security.

## 2. Maritime Governance & International Law (UNCLOS)

### United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS, 1982)
The universal legal framework governing ocean space and maritime sovereignty:
1. **Internal Waters**: Waters landward of the territorial baseline. Full sovereignty; foreign vessels have no right of innocent passage.
2. **Territorial Sea (Up to 12 Nautical Miles from Baseline)**:
   - Coastal state exercises complete sovereignty over air space, water column, seabed, and subsoil.
   - Foreign vessels enjoy the **Right of Innocent Passage** (continuous and expeditious transit that is not prejudicial to the peace, good order, or security of the coastal state).
3. **Contiguous Zone (Up to 24 Nautical Miles)**:
   - Coastal state may exercise control necessary to prevent and punish infringements of its customs, fiscal, immigration, or sanitary laws.
4. **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ, Up to 200 Nautical Miles)**:
   - Coastal state has **sovereign rights for exploring, exploiting, conserving, and managing natural resources** (living fisheries, non-living hydrocarbons, seabed minerals, wind/wave energy).
   - Foreign states enjoy freedoms of navigation and overflight, and laying of submarine cables and pipelines.
5. **Continental Shelf (Up to 350 Nautical Miles / 200 NM minimum)**: Sovereign rights over non-living seabed and mineral resources.
6. **High Seas**: International waters open to all states; designated as the **"Common Heritage of Mankind"** under the International Seabed Authority (ISA).

### The Indo-Pacific Paradigm & The South China Sea Dispute
- **Indo-Pacific Construct**: An integrated geopolitical and economic theater linking the Indian and Pacific Oceans, replacing the narrower "Asia-Pacific" concept.
- **South China Sea Arbitration (Philippines v. China, 2016, Permanent Court of Arbitration)**:
  - Ruled that China's historical "Nine-Dash Line" claim has no legal basis under UNCLOS.
  - India stands for a **Free, Open, Inclusive, and Rules-Based Indo-Pacific (FOIP)**, demanding unrestricted freedom of navigation, overflight, and adherence to international law (UNCLOS 1982) without coercion.

## 3. Plurilateral Groupings & Global Governance

### The Quad (Quadrilateral Security Dialogue)
- Members: India, United States, Japan, Australia.
- Not a military alliance (not an "Asian NATO"); operates as a diplomatic and functional cooperative forum.
- Key Initiatives:
  - **Indo-Pacific Partnership for Maritime Domain Awareness (IPMDA)**: Real-time, satellite-based tracking of dark shipping and illegal, unreported, and unregulated (IUU) fishing across the Pacific and Indian Oceans.
  - Quad Vaccine Partnership, Quad Critical and Emerging Technologies Working Group (semiconductor supply chain resilience, 5G diversification), Quad Climate Working Group.

### I2U2 (The "West Asian Quad")
- Members: India, Israel, United Arab Emirates (UAE), United States.
- Focus: Mobilizing private sector capital and expertise across 6 identified focus areas: **Water, Energy, Transportation, Space, Health, and Food Security**.
- Flagship projects: UAE investment of \$2 billion in integrated agricultural food parks in Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh; 300 MW hybrid renewable energy project in Dwarka, Gujarat.

### Expanded BRICS (BRICS+)
- Founded by Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa (2009).
- Historic Expansion (2024): Admitted Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia, and the United Arab Emirates.
- Strategic Focus: De-dollarization and local currency trade settlements; expansion of the **New Development Bank (NDB)** headquartered in Shanghai; reform of the Bretton Woods institutions (IMF and World Bank quotas).

### G20: New Delhi Leaders' Declaration (2023)
- Under India's Presidency themed *"Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam"* ("One Earth, One Family, One Future"):
  - **Induction of the African Union (AU)** as a permanent member of the G20, elevating the voice of the Global South.
  - **India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC)**: Strategic multimodal connectivity infrastructure linking India to Europe via the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Jordan, and Israel.
  - Launch of the **Global Biofuels Alliance (GBA)**.
  - Tripling of global renewable energy capacity by 2030 target.

### World Trade Organization (WTO) Crisis & Indian Stance
- **Paralysis of the Dispute Settlement Body (DSB)**:
  - The Appellate Body has been non-functional since December 2019 due to the United States blocking judicial appointments to the 7-member bench.
- **Food Security & Public Stockholding**:
  - India defends its right to procure foodgrains at Minimum Support Price (MSP) under the **Peace Clause** (agreed at Bali Ministerial 2013), protecting public stockholding programs for food security from legal challenge under the WTO Agreement on Agriculture (which caps trade-distorting farm subsidies at 10% of total agricultural production for developing nations).
